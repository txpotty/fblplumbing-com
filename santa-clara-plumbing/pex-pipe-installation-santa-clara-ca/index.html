    Copper to PEX Conversions in Santa Clara

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. A switch to PEX ends it. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Santa Clara

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Both last decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Santa Clara

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. There is no crimp gauge to check. Because the fittings are wide inside, each joint costs very little flow.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Santa Clara

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating loops need oxygen-barrier PEX, which has a coating that keeps oxygen from migrating through the wall and rusting the boiler, the pump and any iron in the system. Loop length matters as well. Run them too long and the last stretch never warms up.
