    Sump Pump Types We Replace in Santa Clara, CA

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Santa Clara

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. A check valve that leaks lets the discharge column fall back into the pit, so the pump runs again for the same water and wears itself out early. New valve every time. Vertical switches suit tight pits, and tethered floats suit wide ones.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Santa Clara

    Horsepower matters less than people think. A third-horse pump moves plenty of water for most homes, as long as the vertical lift to the discharge is under about 15 feet. We check the flow chart against your actual lift before we pick a size. Oversize it and it short cycles.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Santa Clara, CA

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We replace the pump and float rod and rerun the PVC up to the check valve. They usually cost less than a submersible.

    Combination Sump Pump System Replacement in Santa Clara, CA

    Combination units put a primary AC pump and a DC backup on one discharge manifold, so you get two pumps and two floats in a single assembly. They are handy when the basin is too small for two pumps side by side. We replace the whole assembly or just the failed side.

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Santa Clara, CA

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We swap in a corrosion-resistant pump and clear out leaves and silt. Discharge goes away from the foundation. Test them each spring.

    Sump Basin and Pit Replacement in Santa Clara, CA

    Old clay tile or 18-inch plastic basins crack and fill with silt, and a pump in a basin that small cycles every few minutes during a heavy rain. The fix is to cut the slab, drop in a bigger poly basin with a bolted lid, and patch the concrete. More volume in the pit means fewer starts per hour. Fewer starts, longer motor life.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Santa Clara

    Basement bathrooms below the sewer line drain into a sealed, vented basin, and the ejector pump in that basin has to pass 2-inch solids. That is not a sump pump job. The ejector and check valve get replaced, then we reseal the lid and check the vent connection. A bad seal shows up as sewer odor.
