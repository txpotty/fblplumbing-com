    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Salinas

    The cost of water heater installation in Salinas, CA can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any necessary plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units generally have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    A standard tank replacement typically takes two to four hours. Tankless installs or relocations can take most of a day.

    FBL Plumbing installs all major reputable brands of water heaters, including Rheem, Bradford White, A.O. Smith and Noritz. We work with both gas and electric models, as well as many sizes and efficiencies. Our team can help you choose the best brand and model to suit your hot water demands and budget.

    Most standard water heater installations, especially for replacing a similar tank unit, can be completed within 3 to 4 hours. However, a conversion from a tank to a tankless system, or installations requiring significant plumbing or electrical modifications, might take a full day or longer. We will provide an estimated timeline during our first assessment.

    Common signs that you might need a new water heater include a lack of hot water, rusty or discolored water coming from the hot tap, banging or rumbling noises from the tank, visible leaks around the base of the unit and an age of 10 to 15 years or more. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspection.

    The average lifespan of a traditional tank water heater is about 10 to 15 years, while tankless water heaters can last 20 years or more with proper maintenance. Factors like local water quality, maintenance frequency and usage habits can all affect the actual lifespan of your unit. Regular flushing can help extend its life.

    The correct size water heater depends on the number of people in your household and your peak hot water demand. For tank units, a 40 to 50 gallon tank is common for 2 to 4 people, while larger families might need 60 to 80 gallons. For tankless units, it is about flow rate (GPM) and temperature rise. Our plumbers will assess your needs to recommend the right size.
