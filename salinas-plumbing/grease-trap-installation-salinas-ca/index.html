    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Salinas

    Small indoor hydromechanical traps usually cost $1,500 to $5,000 installed. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more with excavation, concrete and site restoration. We provide a written estimate after a site visit.

    We calculate flow from every connected fixture, including sinks, floor sinks and dish machines. Hydromechanical units are typically rated by gallons per minute, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time.

    We install a variety of reputable grease trap brands, including Zurn, Big Dipper, Schier, and Rockford Separators. Our plumbers are familiar with the specifications and installation requirements of numerous manufacturers, making sure you get a high-quality, durable system tailored to your needs.

    The main purpose of a grease trap is to intercept fats, oils, and greases (FOG) from commercial kitchen wastewater before they enter the public sewer system. This prevents pipe blockages, protects municipal infrastructure, and helps businesses comply with environmental regulations.

    Grease trap installation is a complex commercial plumbing task that requires specialized knowledge, tools, and adherence to strict local codes. Attempting a DIY installation can lead to costly errors, code violations, and potential health hazards. It is always best to hire licensed and experienced plumbers like FBL Plumbing.

    We install all common types of grease traps, including gravity grease interceptors, hydro-mechanical grease traps (automatic grease removal units), and compact under-sink grease traps. Our team helps you choose the best system for your commercial kitchen's specific needs and local code requirements.

    While the terms are often used interchangeably, 'grease trap' typically refers to smaller, indoor hydro-mechanical units, often found under sinks. 'Grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor gravity-fed units with higher capacities. Both serve the same purpose of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Determining the correct grease trap size involves calculating your kitchen's wastewater flow rate, fixture count, and peak operating hours. Our experienced plumbers will conduct a complete assessment of your Salinas business to recommend the appropriate size and type of grease trap that meets local codes and your operational needs.
