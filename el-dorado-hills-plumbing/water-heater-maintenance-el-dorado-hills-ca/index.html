    During a water heater flush, we drain the tank completely and then refill it, letting water flow out through the drain valve until it runs clear. This process removes mineral deposits and sediment that collect at the bottom of the tank, which can hurt efficiency and cause damage.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual maintenance for your water heater. In homes with harder water, flushing every 6 to 12 months can be helpful to prevent sediment buildup.

    While some basic tasks like flushing the tank might seem DIY-friendly, professional maintenance makes sure all critical components, such as the anode rod, pressure relief valve, and heating elements, are properly inspected. Incorrect DIY methods can damage your unit or void warranties.

    The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that sacrifices itself to keep the steel tank from corroding. It attracts corrosive elements in the water. Regular inspection and replacement of a depleted anode rod are key for extending the lifespan of your water heater, especially where water quality varies.
