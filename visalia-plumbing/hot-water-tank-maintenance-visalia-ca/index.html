    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Visalia

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. That alone takes 30 to 40 minutes. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Small leaks at the dielectric nipples get noted on your report.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Visalia, CA

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Visalia

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve. Some models hide the anode under the top cover. Bradford White often uses a combo anode in the hot outlet nipple. We carry the right sockets for each brand.

    Rental and Commercial Tank Logs in Visalia

    Landlords and property managers with a dozen units need to know which tank is next to fail. Each tank we touch goes into a log. We service 30 to 100 gallon tanks in rentals.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Visalia, CA

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. If six inches of wire is bare, it is time for a new one.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Visalia, CA

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. Draft at the hood gets checked first. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.
