    Drips that come back after a repair are the clearest sign. Corrosion on the body, gritty handles, a wobbly base and low flow that survives an aerator cleaning all say the faucet is near the end. Sometimes it is just looks.

    We work on every major brand. Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard and Pfister make up most of the faucets we replace, and we know their mounting hardware and cartridges by sight. We handle off-brand models as well.

    Look under the sink first. Stains on the cabinet floor and crust on the connections usually mean the body leaks, and that is a replacement. Then look at the spout. A cracked body or a handle that will not close all the way means replace it.

    That depends on what is under your sink. If the stops are newer quarter-turn valves and the old faucet comes off without a fight, it is a reasonable DIY job, but older homes often have corroded stops and fused nuts that make it much harder. We back the labor with a warranty.

    Worn cartridges and O-rings cause most leaks. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe.
