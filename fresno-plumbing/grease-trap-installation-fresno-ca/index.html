    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Installation in Fresno, CA

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Fresno, CA

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. Long runs to the trap let grease cool and coat the line, so we keep the trap as close to the fixtures as we can. Closer is better. We also check that a pump truck can reach an outdoor tank with about 100 feet of hose, since a tank the hauler cannot reach costs you more on every service.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Fresno

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. Before anything ships, we check that the tank and covers carry an H-20 traffic rating where trucks will roll over them. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Fresno, CA

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. Licensed home bakeries and houses on old clay laterals that keep clogging are the usual cases. A small 4 to 7 GPM unit under the kitchen sink catches most of it, and cleaning takes about ten minutes with a spatula and a bucket. Check with your local health office first.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Setup in Fresno, CA

    Small cafes and coffee bars often get by with a 10 to 25 GPM hydromechanical trap tucked under the prep sink or beside it. The unit goes on the floor or in a shallow recess, and the flow control fitting goes upstream. Clearance matters most. Your staff needs to lift the lid and clean it weekly, so we leave space to do that without unbolting anything.

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Fresno

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. A 50 GPM trap on a line that sees 75 GPM will pass grease every lunch rush. We do the math on paper. Each sink compartment's volume, the drain size and a one or two minute drain time give us the GPM figure the code uses.

    Grease Trap Installation for Schools and Institutions in Fresno

    School cafeterias and hospital kitchens cook in big batches and wash in big batches, which means large pot sinks and a dish machine running hard for two hours after each meal. Most end up with an outdoor gravity interceptor. We schedule around the calendar. School jobs fit into breaks, and hospital jobs get phased so meals keep going out.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Fresno

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Commercial Kitchen Grease Trap Installs in Fresno

    Every commercial install starts with a fixture list. We count each sink, floor drain and dish machine that will tie in, then add the gallons per minute each one can dump. That number decides between an indoor unit and an outdoor tank, and where it can go with clean pipe runs to the sewer. Fewer bends, fewer clogs.
