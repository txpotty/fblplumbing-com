    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Fresno

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in Fresno

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Fresno

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Fresno

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Every suspect part then gets tested with a meter.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Fresno

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. A high whistle is different. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Fresno, CA

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. We read the code, then test the thermopile and sensors before blaming the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting.
