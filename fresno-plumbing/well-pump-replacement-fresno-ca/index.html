    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, usually less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has its own applications and benefits.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job that involves high voltage electricity, heavy equipment and possibly deep well access.     If your pressure tank is old or waterlogged, replacing it at the same time can protect the new pump. A failed tank causes rapid short cycling, which is one of the most frequent reasons submersible motors burn out early.

    Most new well pumps come with a manufacturer's warranty, usually ranging from 1 to 5 years, covering defects in materials and workmanship. FBL Plumbing also backs our installation work with our own labor warranty for complete peace of mind.

    Common signs include no water from your faucets, weak or inconsistent water pressure, unusual noises from the well or pressure tank area, short cycling where the pump turns on and off frequently, or cloudy or muddy water.     Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is usually 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water.
