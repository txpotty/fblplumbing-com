    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Fresno

    Residential ejector systems in Fresno serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Fresno, CA

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. The panel switches the lead pump each cycle so wear is shared, and if the lead pump fails or the flow is heavy, the second pump starts automatically and the panel trips an alarm so someone knows to call for service. Two pumps, one alarm.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Fresno, CA

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Every lid gets sealed.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Fresno

    Macerating systems like Saniflo add a bath fixture or two without breaking the concrete floor. It sits behind the toilet, grinds waste and pumps it through a small line, often three-quarter or one inch, to a drain above, which suits a small half bath. They need a vent too.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Fresno, CA

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. If the basin is sound, we can swap in a better pump, replace the old swing check with a new one, add a full-port shutoff and install a high water alarm, which covers most of what a new system offers for a fraction of the cost. We tell you if the basin is done.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Fresno

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. Sump pumps push groundwater out to the yard, ejectors push sewage to the sewer, and only the ejector pit must be sealed and vented against gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Fresno

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We cut the slab, trench the drain lines to the basin, set it, run a two-inch vent up through the house to the roof and pipe the discharge to the building drain with a check valve and shutoff. Then we patch the concrete.
