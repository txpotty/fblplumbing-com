    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Fremont

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    Both work well in Northern California homes. PEX bends, tolerates freezes and costs less. Copper is rigid, lasts a long time and tolerates sunlight and heat, and we will help you choose based on your home, your water and your budget.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It is not a weekend job. Hidden leaks, failed inspections and voided warranties are common results of DIY repipes, so this one is best left to licensed plumbers.

    Some disruption is unavoidable, but most families stay home the whole time. Expect daytime shutoffs. We cover floors and furniture, clean up every evening and turn the water back on before we leave, so you can still shower, cook and run the dishwasher at night.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Big houses need more time. Fixture count, attic access and pipe choice set the schedule, and you will usually have water each evening.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.
