    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Fremont

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, and they hold temperature off thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. Our tech cleans the screen, checks sensor values in the service menu and tests thermistor resistance against the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Navien Condensate & Fan Repair in Fremont

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and it usually traces to scale or a backed-up condensate line. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. We clean the trap and screen, then retest.

    Tankless Water Heater Descaling Service in Fremont

    Scale is calcium and magnesium carbonate that comes out of hard water as it heats up, and a tankless exchanger heats water fast through narrow passages. Rural properties around Fremont often rely on private wells, which may need filtration for iron, sediment, or other minerals. Our tech connects a pump to the isolation valves and runs descaler through the exchanger for close to an hour. Then we flush with clean water. The inlet screen gets cleaned too.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Repair in Fremont, CA

    Whole-house electric tankless units pull a lot of current, often 100 to 150 amps on two or three dedicated breakers. A breaker that trips under load can mean a shorted element or a loose lug that has been arcing, and both get tested with a meter before anything is replaced. Element resistance and supply voltage get measured. Scorched wire gets cut back.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Fremont, CA

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. Our tech cleans the burner and fan, checks every vent joint, and compares the flow sensor reading to the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Fremont, CA

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Fremont

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just at the moment it lights. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, because pressure that drops below the manufacturer's minimum under load causes flame loss and cold slugs. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Fremont, CA

    Rinnai units are common, and scale causes most of their service calls. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and we test every one against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Fremont

    Expect somewhere between $150 and $800 for most tankless repairs in Fremont, based on the part and the time it takes to find the fault. Sensors are cheap. Heat exchangers and control boards are not. We give a written estimate after testing.

    Codes fire when a sensor reads something outside its range: low flow, no flame signal, high exhaust temperature or a blocked vent. Each one maps to a specific test.

    Temperature swings, hot water that cuts out partway through a shower and any code on the display are the usual ones. A rotten egg smell near a gas unit means leave the house and call the gas company.

    Our labor warranty appears on the invoice, and factory parts carry the manufacturer's own coverage on top of it. Ask for the terms before we start and we will spell them out.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. Next on the list are blocked air intakes, dirty flame rods and failed flow sensors, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of it is preventable.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen two or three times a year. Putting a softener or scale cartridge upstream cuts scale buildup a great deal, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    With yearly descaling, 15 to 20 years is common on gas tankless heaters. Skip maintenance on hard water and trouble can start in three to five years.

    Most take one to three hours on site. Descaling alone is closer to an hour and a half, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. You will know before the tech leaves.
