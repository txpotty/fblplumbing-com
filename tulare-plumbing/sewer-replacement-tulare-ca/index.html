    Sewer Line Repair vs. Full Replacement in Tulare

    We look at the whole line before recommending either. A single bad joint can usually be dug and fixed. Breaks every few feet, roots at every joint, or a belly that holds water for twenty feet tell us the line is failing everywhere, and in that case a full replacement saves you from paying to dig the same yard twice.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement Options in Tulare, CA

    Bursting and pulling replace the pipe. Lining patches the inside of the old one. We use bursting when the old line is broken or undersized, because it leaves you with brand new pipe rather than a liner inside an old one, and lining when the pipe is still round and mostly intact. Each has its place.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Tulare, CA

    Most house laterals run 30 to 100 feet. Depth varies a lot. We measure both before quoting, check whether the run passes under a driveway, porch or tree, then pick trench or trenchless based on what is in the way, so the estimate you sign is the price you pay.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Tulare

    Bursting works best on clay, cast iron and Orangeburg. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind which leaves a single joint-free pipe in the ground. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.

    Code-Compliant Sewer Replacement in Tulare

    Code sets the minimum slope, the cleanout spacing and the pipe type. Inspectors check all three in an open trench. We set fall at a quarter inch per foot unless the run requires otherwise, place cleanouts at the house and at major direction changes, and only bury pipe rated for it. Paperwork stays on file.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Tulare, CA

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. They need only a tiny gap. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and a root cutter clears them for a season at most, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.
