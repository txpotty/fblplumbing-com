    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Tulare

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. A licensed crew finishes faster and provides warranties and documentation.

    Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. It adds scale. Mineral scale cuts flow and adds strain to brittle pipe, which is why a softener loop during the repipe can make sense in hard water.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. We recommend materials based on your home and water conditions.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Terms are on your estimate. Certified PEX-A installs also carry the pipe manufacturer's long-term warranty, and we register it for you so the paperwork is on file if you ever need it.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Some insurers raise rates or decline coverage for homes with polybutylene.
