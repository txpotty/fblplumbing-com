    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Oakland

    Navien condensing units run a second stainless exchanger that pulls heat from the exhaust. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. We clear the trap, check the PVC vent pitch and test the circulation pump on NPE-A models with built-in recirculation. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Tankless Repair vs Replacement Cost in Oakland, CA

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350, and sensor or igniter repairs land between $250 and $600. A heat exchanger out of warranty can cost $800 to $1,500 in parts and labor. A new unit costs a lot more. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Oakland

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just when it first fires. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, since pressure that sags under load leads to flame loss and slugs of cold water. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Tankless Error Code Diagnostics in Oakland, CA

    Every tankless brand flashes its own codes, so a code 11 on one unit means something else on another. A Rinnai 11 is no ignition, a Navien E003 is ignition failure, and a Noritz 11 is also an ignition fault, but the tests behind them differ. We read the code, pull the history and test the part tied to it. Guessing gets expensive.

    No Hot Water Tankless Repair in Oakland

    No hot water at all usually points to ignition, gas supply or a heat exchanger choked with scale. We start with the error history in the service menu, then test the igniter, flame rod and gas pressure in that order, because a weak flame signal and low pressure can look identical on the display. We look at the vent run as well. Common igniters and flame rods ride on the truck.

    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Oakland

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, then trim the burner using thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. We pull the screen, read sensor values in the service menu and compare thermistor resistance to the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Repair in Oakland

    Restaurants and laundromats in Oakland often run two to six tankless units on a common manifold. When one unit drops out, the rest carry the load and scale up sooner, so we check the cascade controller, the isolation valves on each heater and the water quality coming in. Downtime costs money. We schedule around service hours when we can.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Repair in Oakland, CA

    Whole-house electric tankless units pull a lot of current, often 100 to 150 amps on two or three dedicated breakers. A breaker that trips under load can mean a shorted element or a loose lug that has been arcing, and both get tested with a meter before anything is replaced. We check element resistance and supply voltage. Scorched wire gets cut back.
