    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Oakland, CA

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall flattens slowly under the dirt above it, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Sewer Line Material Upgrades in Oakland

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all failed for different reasons. Modern PVC does not rust, flatten or let roots in at glued joints. Schedule 40 or SDR 35 PVC is what most codes call for on a lateral, choosing the wall thickness based on how deep it sits and what drives over it. Glued joints stay sealed.

    Code-Compliant Sewer Replacement in Oakland

    Code sets the minimum slope, the cleanout spacing and the pipe type. Inspectors check all three in an open trench. We set fall at a quarter inch per foot unless the run requires otherwise, place cleanouts at the house and at major direction changes, and only bury pipe rated for it. Paperwork stays on file.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Oakland

    Bursting works best on clay, cast iron and Orangeburg. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind so the new pipe has no joints at all between the pits. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.

    Sewer Cleanout Installation in Oakland, CA

    A lot of older houses have no outside cleanout. Every clog then means pulling a toilet. When we replace a sewer line we add a two-way cleanout near the foundation, bringing a capped riser up to grade where you can reach it, so future cleaning or camera work is quick and cheap.

    Emergency Sewer Line Replacement in Oakland, CA

    Sewage coming up a floor drain or tub is an emergency. Stop running water in the house right away. We open the cleanout to relieve the backup, run a camera to find the break, and when the pipe has caved in, we dig to that spot immediately, putting in a section of new pipe to get you flowing before planning the full replacement.
