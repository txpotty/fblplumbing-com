    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Antioch

    The anode rod is a sacrificial part in your water heater designed to corrode instead of the tank itself. Depending on your water chemistry, these rods can wear down faster than expected. During maintenance, we inspect the anode rod's condition and recommend replacement if necessary. A healthy anode rod is key for preventing rust and extending the lifespan of your water heater tank, saving you from a costly full replacement.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Maintenance in Antioch

    The cost of water heater maintenance in Antioch, CA can range from $120 to $250, based on the type of water heater (tank or tankless) and the services required. This typically includes a full flush, anode rod inspection, and safety checks. More complex issues or parts replacement would be an additional cost.

    Popping or rumbling noises, lukewarm water, rusty or smelly hot water, and slow recovery times are common signs.

    The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that sacrifices itself to keep the steel tank from corroding. It attracts corrosive elements in the water. Regular inspection and replacement of a depleted anode rod are key for extending the lifespan of your water heater, especially where water quality varies.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual maintenance for your water heater. In homes with harder water, flushing every 6 to 12 months can be beneficial to prevent sediment buildup.

    Many manufacturers require regular maintenance to keep warranties valid, especially for tankless units. We provide service records for your records.

    With hard water, flushing once a year is recommended. Homes with very hard water or no softener may benefit from flushing every six months.

    Most tank flushes and inspections take about 45 to 90 minutes. Tankless descaling usually takes 1 to 2 hours.

    Sediment buildup consists of mineral deposits, mainly calcium and magnesium, that settle at the bottom of your water heater tank. Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running mineral-heavy. Sediment cuts efficiency, causes strange noises, and can lead to premature tank failure if not flushed regularly.
