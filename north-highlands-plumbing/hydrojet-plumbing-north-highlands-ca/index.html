    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in North Highlands

    Residential hydrojetting in North Highlands typically costs $400 to $900, including a camera inspection before and after. Snaking costs less per visit. Fewer repeat visits often make jetting the cheaper choice over time.

    A snake bores a hole through the clog. Jetting cleans the whole pipe. The snake leaves grease, scale and root fibers on the pipe walls where they catch the next clog, while jetting removes that buildup so the line stays clear far longer.

    It is safe for most sound PVC, ABS, cast iron and clay. Settings change by material. Thin-wall pipe, old Orangeburg fiber pipe and any line with cracks or separated joints get lower pressure or no jetting at all, which is why the camera inspection happens before we ever turn the jetter on.

    Most homes in North Highlands only need jetting every one to three years, or when clogs start returning. Busy kitchens need more. Grease lines often need quarterly service, and root-prone sewers usually go a year or two.

    We guarantee the line will flow freely when we leave. The service warranty is written on your invoice. If the camera shows a defect that could cause a clog to return, such as a belly or a root-filled crack, we point it out before we start so the terms are clear and nobody is surprised later.

    Usually, if the pipe is sound. The camera tells us. Clay, cast iron and even some Orangeburg can be jetted at reduced pressure when the walls are intact, but a pipe with cracks, missing sections or separated joints should be repaired rather than jetted.

    Call us within the guarantee period and we come back. Usually there is a reason. A clog that returns soon after a thorough jetting usually means a defect in the pipe, like a belly, an offset joint or a crack letting roots in, and the camera footage from the first visit tells us where to look.

    Rental jetters exist, but they are risky without training. They can cause real injuries. Used on a cracked pipe, a jetter can also make the break worse, and without a camera you will not know the condition of the line before you start or whether the clog actually cleared.
