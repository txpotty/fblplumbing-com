    Basement Wall Sealing in North Highlands, CA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge.     Waterproofing for Older Homes in North Highlands, CA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Copper became standard in many mid-century homes here, and decades later pinhole leaks and corroded fittings are a common repair call. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products.     Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in North Highlands

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in North Highlands

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in North Highlands

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. A full exterior dig and membrane on a basement usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000, depending on depth, wall length and what sits against the house, while interior drain tile with a sump typically costs $4,000 to $12,000. Access changes the price a lot. We itemize every quote after the inspection.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, with drain tile along the footing and a sump to pump it out. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. The exterior route costs more.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. Done wrong, it can trap water against the wall and cause more damage.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on weather and how deep the footing sits. We give you a timeline at the first visit.

    Look low on the wall first. Damp patches, a musty smell, mold on the bottom of the drywall, a white powder on block or brick called efflorescence, and puddles after rain all point to water coming through. Bowing walls and stair-step cracks need a closer look too.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, so the studs and sheathing rot faster than if the wall had been left alone. The leak gets repaired and the cavity dried before anything else.

    It depends on the wall and the site. On exterior foundation work we use rubberized asphalt sheet membranes, polyurethane-modified asphalt liquids, bentonite panels and crystalline coatings, picking the one that suits the soil and the wall material. For showers, we use Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, and that is what a basement sitting in wet soil or a high water table calls for.
