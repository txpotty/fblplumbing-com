    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in North Highlands

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    Plumbing contractors here must hold a state contractor license, and permits are typically required for water heaters, repiping, and sewer replacements. In many areas, a plumbing permit is required for new sump pump installations or significant replacements so the work meets local building codes. FBL Plumbing knows local permitting requirements in North Highlands and can pull the necessary permits so your installation is compliant.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.

    We install a wide range of high-quality, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you choose the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your North Highlands home, considering horsepower and features.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to make sure the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps catch issues early.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will match the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit typically takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching typically takes most of a day.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also smart to consider a replacement before it fails completely.
