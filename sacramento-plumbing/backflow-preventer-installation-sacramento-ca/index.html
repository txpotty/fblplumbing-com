    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Sacramento

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules permit. We size them to avoid excessive pressure loss on your system.

    Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in Sacramento

    The double check valve assembly, or DCVA, is a widely used backflow preventer for low-hazard applications where the consequences of backflow are not severe.     Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Installation in Sacramento, CA

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of protection and are required for high hazard connections like commercial kitchens, medical facilities, chemical injection and car washes. They include two independent check valves with a relief valve that dumps water if either check fails. Because the relief valve can discharge, we plan for drainage and install the assembly above grade where it can be tested easily. We carry proven brands like Watts, Febco, Wilkins and Apollo.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Assembly Installation in Sacramento

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of backflow protection and are often required for high-hazard applications in Sacramento. These devices feature two independent check valves and a relief valve that opens to discharge water if pressure differentials indicate a backflow condition. FBL Plumbing focuses on precise RPZ installation, with proper sizing, placement and testing to meet all regulatory requirements for residential and commercial properties.     Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Sacramento

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Sacramento, CA typically ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is usually on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    In Sacramento, most backflow preventers must be tested once a year by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is essential for ongoing compliance and safety.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for new backflow assemblies, especially on commercial properties. We check local rules and pull the permit when needed.

    We commonly install high-quality backflow preventers from trusted brands such as Watts, Wilkins by Zurn, Apollo and Conbraco. Our plumbers are experienced with a wide range of devices and can recommend the right option for your application and budget.

    Constant dripping or discharge from an RPZ relief valve, low pressure downstream and a failed annual test are common signs. Visible corrosion or a cracked body from freezing also means service is needed.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice comes down to the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.

    Most manufacturers offer one to five year warranties depending on the model. We also warranty our installation labor and will go over the coverage with you.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and boilers.
