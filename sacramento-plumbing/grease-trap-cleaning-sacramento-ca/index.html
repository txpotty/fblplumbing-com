    Grease Trap Inspection and Repair in Sacramento

    An empty trap is the only time anyone can see what is wrong with it. We look for cracked baffles and missing outlet tees. Without an outlet tee, the grease cap flows right into the sewer, and older concrete interceptors lose that tee more often than you would think. Small fixes can often happen the same visit.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Sacramento, CA

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. We open every compartment and pump each one to the floor.     Preventive Maintenance Programs in Sacramento, CA

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. Your manager does not have to remember it, because we track the dates. If the sludge judge shows the trap hitting 20 percent between visits, we pull the next date in, and if it reads 10 percent, we push it out and save you a pump-out. Adjustments happen at every visit.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Sacramento

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Some cities set a fixed interval instead, often 90 days for interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.

    Grease Line Jetting in Sacramento

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. The buildup forms rings that are thickest along the bottom. Snaking just bores a small path that closes up in weeks, but a rotating jetter nozzle strips grease off the full wall of the pipe. A camera confirms it afterward.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Sacramento, CA

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.
