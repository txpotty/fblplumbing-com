    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Sacramento, CA

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. A single slab leak repair can run $1,000 to $4,000 once access and restoration are included. The numbers add up quickly. By the second or third leak, a repipe usually pays for itself, and we put both options side by side so you can decide.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Sacramento, CA

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Weak flow and rusty water are common. We remove or abandon the galvanized lines and replace them with PEX or copper, and your water pressure and water quality usually improve the same day the new lines go into service.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Sacramento, CA

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Brown water after the house sits overnight, weak flow at upstairs showers and leaks at threaded joints are the usual signs that galvanized is near the end. We replace it with PEX or copper. Most homeowners notice the pressure difference the first time they turn on the shower.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Sacramento, CA

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Sacramento

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Many older homes here sit on raised foundations with crawlspaces, which make supply and drain repairs more accessible than slab work. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. It ends this leak and takes the rest of the slab plumbing out of the picture.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Sacramento, CA

    Rural properties around Sacramento often rely on private wells, which may need filtration for iron, sediment, or other minerals. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Adding the loop now costs less than later.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Sacramento, CA

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. Copper commonly costs 20 to 40 percent more than PEX, and some owners prefer it anyway for its long history. Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Sacramento

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. We can usually identify it from the pipe at the water heater, and full replacement makes the house easier to insure and sell.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most Northern California jurisdictions. We pull the permit and book the inspections.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX costs less, installs faster, handles freezing better and runs quietly, while copper tolerates sunlight and heat better and appeals to owners who want metal pipe, so the right pick depends on your house.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It takes skill and time.
