    Replacing a pump generally does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they are needed.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less noticeable. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and often more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained annually, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and confirming the discharge line is clear.     A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal.
