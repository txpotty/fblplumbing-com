    Softener Brands We Work On in Vallejo

    Most softeners sold in the last 20 years run on a handful of valve platforms, mainly Fleck, Clack, Autotrol and a few proprietary heads. Once you know the valve, the name on the cabinet matters less. Kinetico and Culligan build their own valves, so we check part availability before quoting.

    Bypass Valve and Drain Line Repairs in Vallejo, CA

    A bypass valve that does not seat fully lets raw water blend into the softened supply, and the hardness test is usually the first clue. The bypass gets rebuilt or swapped out and check the drain line for kinks. The drain line has to end above its receptor with an air gap.

    Hardness Testing at the Tap in Vallejo

    We test with a titration kit, counting drops until the sample changes color, because test strips are too rough for setting a valve. Raw water gets tested from an unsoftened hose bib or with the valve on bypass. Softened water comes from the kitchen tap.

    Regeneration Programming and Hardness Testing in Vallejo, CA

    Plenty of softeners still run factory settings. The installer never changed them, or the household grew and nobody updated the valve. We enter your raw hardness and household size, then set a metered valve to regenerate by gallons used instead of every few days on a timer.

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Vallejo, CA

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. It does not take much debris to block it. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 if they are worn.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Vallejo, CA

    Plans take the remembering off your plate. We call ahead of each yearly visit, and a valve failure between visits moves you to the front of the line. Repairs found during a plan visit are billed at a reduced rate.

    Resin Rebedding in Vallejo

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we empty out the old resin and refill the tank with new high-capacity media, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Resin Cleaning and Regeneration Tuning in Vallejo, CA

    Iron is the most common resin fouler we see. It coats the beads orange and blocks the exchange sites. We feed a resin cleaner through the brine well, run a regeneration and retest, and if the capacity comes back we shorten the time between regenerations so the iron does not build back up.
