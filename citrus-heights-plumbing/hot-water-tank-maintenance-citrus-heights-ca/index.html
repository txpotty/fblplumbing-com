    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Citrus Heights, CA

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. Gas units get a burner, thermocouple and vent check. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Electric Element Check in Citrus Heights

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Sediment Flushing in Citrus Heights, CA

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. Our method is short bursts of line pressure that churn up the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Electric Thermostat Service in Citrus Heights

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. A tripped reset is a symptom. We trace the cause, often a shorted element or a stuck thermostat, before resetting it. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Citrus Heights

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve. Some models hide the anode under the top cover. Bradford White often uses a combo anode in the hot outlet nipple. We carry the right sockets for each brand.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Citrus Heights, CA

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We put in an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Citrus Heights

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. A gauge on a hose bib tells us the static pressure. Then we lift the relief valve lever. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.
