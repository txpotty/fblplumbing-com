    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Rancho Cordova

    Most new single-family homes in the Rancho Cordova area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. Each added bathroom adds several thousand dollars. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. Have them delivered before trim so we can open each box, since a missing part or wrong finish can delay trim a week.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling fixtures or sewer odor point to venting or slope problems. We can camera the line from the cleanout to look for a belly or a bad joint while the builder warranty still applies. Construction debris is common.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It flexes and needs fewer fittings in the walls than copper. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. Copper still shows up at water heaters.

    We handle the plumbing permit work and inspections on our scope. Your builder usually pulls the main building permit. Our office then schedules each plumbing inspection, meets the inspector on site, and fixes any correction notice before the next trade needs that wall or slab.

    We can run a dedicated return line during rough-in. Hot water then reaches distant bathrooms in seconds. Compared with a crossover valve at the sink, a dedicated return keeps the cold line cold and works faster. The pump needs an outlet.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. First comes underground, with drains holding a water or air test before the pour. The rough or top-out inspection happens before insulation and drywall, and the final comes once fixtures are set, the water heater is running and the gas is tested. Some add a gas test.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drain, waste and vent runs are PVC or ABS. Cast iron is still used in some multifamily buildings and in walls next to bedrooms where drain noise matters, since it is much quieter than plastic when a toilet flushes upstairs.
