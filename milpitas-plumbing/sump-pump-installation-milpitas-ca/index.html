    Zoeller Sump Pump Installation in Milpitas, CA

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Milpitas

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi monitors go further and send a phone alert when the pump runs too long, the power drops or the water climbs past a set level. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Milpitas

    No pit yet? We cut one at the low corner. We backfill around the basin with washed gravel so groundwater has a path in. The slab gets patched with new concrete. The pump, lid and discharge go in last.

    Commercial Sump Pump Solutions in Milpitas

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and loading dock drains need pumps rated for more volume. For those jobs we install duplex systems where two pumps alternate, with a control panel and a high water alarm that can tie into the building monitoring. Downtime costs money. We plan the install around your business hours.

    Primary Sump Pump Installation in Milpitas

    A primary sump pump is the one doing the daily work, kicking on every time the float rises in the pit after rain or snowmelt. Sizing comes from two measurements we take on site, the pit diameter and the vertical lift to the discharge. Most homes need 1/3 or 1/2 HP. We usually pair that with a 1-1/2 inch discharge line.

    Crawl Space Sump Pump Installation in Milpitas, CA

    Crawl spaces with standing water breed mold and rot sill plates within a few seasons. A small sump basin at the low point fixes that. Clearance is usually under two feet. We route the discharge out through the rim joist and away from the foundation.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Milpitas, CA

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. That makes service easy. Expect more motor noise than a submersible. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Milpitas, CA

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. Our trucks carry 1/3 and 1/2 HP submersibles, check valves and fittings, so in most homes we can pull the old pump and set a new one in one visit. Figure on two hours for a typical swap. We test it with a full pit before leaving.
