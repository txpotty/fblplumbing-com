    Salt-Based Water Softener Systems in Santa Rosa, CA

    Salt-based softeners use ion exchange, swapping calcium and magnesium for sodium on a bed of resin beads. FBL Plumbing installs these with a properly sized resin tank and brine tank. We program regeneration by metered water use so the system only recharges when the resin is actually spent, which saves a lot of salt. They work best on very hard water.

    Water Softener Installation for New Construction in Santa Rosa, CA

    Whole-Home Water Softener Installation in Santa Rosa

    Whole-home softeners treat every gallon that enters the house, right after the main shutoff. That protects every faucet, shower, water heater and appliance downstream, which is where hard water does most of its damage over the years. Sizing comes first.     High-Efficiency Water Softener Installation in Santa Rosa

    High-efficiency softeners use upflow brining and metered valves to cut salt and water use. Compared with an older timer-based unit, a modern metered softener can use roughly half the salt and noticeably less regeneration water each month. The savings add up. We install several models.

    Fleck valves, especially the 5600SXT and 7000SXT, are on a lot of the softeners we install. Their parts are easy to find and the valves can be rebuilt instead of replaced, so the system lasts. Settings match your test. We confirm the setup before leaving.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Installation in Santa Rosa

    Water softener installation in Santa Rosa, CA usually runs $1,500 to $4,000. System type, brand, capacity and how much plumbing work the install needs all move the price within that range. Loops add cost. You get a written quote after we test.

    Salt use depends on hardness, water use and how efficient the softener is. A family of four with fairly hard water might use 40 to 80 pounds of salt per month, and a metered high-efficiency model uses less. Metered valves save most.

    Adding salt every 4 to 8 weeks is most of the work. A yearly checkup with a plumber catches worn seals, salt bridges in the brine tank and clogged injectors before they cause hard water. Do not let the salt run out.

    White, chalky scale on faucets and dishes is the most common sign. Dry skin after showering, dull laundry, weak lather and lower water pressure from pipe buildup all point the same way. You likely have hard water.

    Softeners remove hardness minerals using salt. Conditioners alter the minerals instead, and softeners are stronger for very hard water.
