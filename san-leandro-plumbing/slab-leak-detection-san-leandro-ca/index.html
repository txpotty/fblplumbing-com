    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in San Leandro

    The cost of slab leak detection in San Leandro, CA typically ranges from $250 to $700, depending on the complexity of the leak and the time required for detection. This cost covers the professional equipment and expertise needed to accurately locate the leak, which is crucial before any repair work can begin.

    Direct repair works well for a single leak on otherwise healthy pipe in an accessible spot. Rerouting is often better under tile or hardwood and avoids future leaks on that line. We help you weigh both.

    Some homeowner's insurance policies may cover the cost of accessing and repairing the slab leak, particularly if the leak was sudden and accidental. However, coverage often varies, and damage caused by long-term neglect might not be covered. We recommend checking your specific policy details or contacting your insurance provider for clarification.

    Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water with fixtures off, a moving water meter and an unexplained jump in your water bill are common signs. Cracked tile, damp carpet and mildew odors also suggest a slab leak. Call us if you notice any of these.

    Common signs of a slab leak in Northern California homes include unexplained increases in water bills, warm spots on your floor, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, damp or mildewy smells and visible cracks in your foundation or walls. Pooling water outside your home near the foundation can also be a clear indicator.

    Many policies cover the cost to access the leak and repair resulting water damage, though the pipe itself may not be covered. Coverage varies by insurer. We provide photos and a written report for your claim.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes vulnerable to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures. Galvanized steel pipes, though less common under slabs today, can also corrode and leak.

    For major slab leak repairs, especially those involving excavation or extensive re-piping, a plumbing permit from the local building department is often required.
