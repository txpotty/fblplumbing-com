    Ejector and Grinder Pump Jobs We Handle in Walnut Creek, CA

    Grinder Pump Installation in Walnut Creek

    Grinder pumps chop waste into a fine slurry first, which lets them push through small pipe over long distances. They are the right choice when the sewer is far away, uphill or under pressure, like on a pressure sewer system where the pump feeds a shared force main in the street. Grinders run pricier. For a basement bath near the drain, a standard ejector usually works.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Walnut Creek, CA

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Every lid gets sealed.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Walnut Creek

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Zoeller Ejector Pump Installation in Walnut Creek, CA

    Zoeller has made ejector pumps for decades, and its cast iron models are the ones we see still running in older basements. We install Zoeller for both new basins and drop-in replacements, and its standard models often fit existing pits and discharge piping without changes. Floats and switches are stocked locally.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Walnut Creek, CA

    Liberty builds simplex and duplex packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Stuck floats are common.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Walnut Creek

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. We still add a shutoff.

Commercial setups often run duplex pumps with an alternating panel, so one pump can fail and the other keeps the restrooms open. Closed restrooms cost business.
