    Gas Leak Detection in San Mateo

    A rotten egg smell, hissing near a line, dead grass in a stripe across the yard or a gas meter that moves with everything off are the common leak signs. Trust your nose. Our plumbers isolate the system section by section, sweep it with an electronic detector and confirm the exact spot with soap solution. After we locate the leak, we price the repair in writing.

    CSST Repair in San Mateo

    Corrugated stainless steel tubing is a flexible gas pipe with a plastic jacket, often yellow, that runs through attics and walls in newer homes. It can be damaged. Common damage comes from nails, rodents or lightning, and repairs require cutting out the bad section and using fittings from the same manufacturer. Bonding gets checked on every system. The system should be bonded to the electrical grounding system with a proper clamp and conductor, per the manufacturer's instructions and local code.

    Black Iron Pipe Repair in San Mateo, CA

    Black iron is the standard interior gas pipe in a lot of homes, and it lasts a long time indoors. Joints are the weak point. Years of vibration from a furnace or a house settling can loosen threaded fittings, and pipe in a damp garage or an exterior wall can rust from the outside in. We reseal or replace the bad sections. Outdoor runs get coated or painted after the repair so rust does not start again.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in San Mateo

    Small fitting or connector repairs usually run $150 to $450. Interior sections cost $400 to $1,200, and buried lines often run $1,000 to $4,000 or higher.

    Utilities lock the meter when they find a leak or a failed test. Service stays off until a licensed plumber fixes it and the system passes a test.

    Simple repairs take an hour or two. Buried yard line replacements often take a full day, plus inspection time if a permit is needed.

    Our gas line repairs come with a written warranty on parts and labor, usually one year. Terms depend on the job. The exact terms are listed on your estimate before work starts, and any new connectors, valves or CSST fittings also carry their manufacturer warranties.

    Gas lines do not need routine service the way a water heater does. A visual check helps, though. During other plumbing visits, we can look over visible gas piping, connectors and shutoffs and point out old brass connectors, missing sediment traps or rust that is worth fixing before it leaks.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Code decides where each one goes. Polyethylene can only run underground and needs a listed riser where it comes up, while CSST needs bonding and black iron needs proper support and gas-rated thread sealant.

    A rotten egg smell is the big one, since utilities add a sulfur odorant to natural gas. Hissing is another sign. Other signs include a hissing sound near a pipe or appliance, dead plants over a buried line or a meter dial that moves with every appliance off, and any of them means leaving the house and calling the utility from outside.

    There is no required yearly inspection for most homes. A look every few years helps older houses. Old brass connectors, decades-old buried steel and appliances with yellow or weak flames are all good reasons to have the system checked.
