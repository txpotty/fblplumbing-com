    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in San Mateo

    Most homeowners in San Mateo, CA pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. A stubborn old toilet adds time. Bolts rusted solid have to be cut with a hacksaw or oscillating tool, and a cast iron flange that breaks during removal needs a repair ring, each of which adds labor.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque. A low flange needs an extender ring, and one extra turn on a nut can split the base, which is why new floors and wall-hung units should go to a pro.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. Gerber and Mansfield are solid on a budget. We match the model to your rough-in and how the bathroom gets used.

    Watch the base and the tank. A puddle that returns after wiping means a crack or a bad seal. We rule out the seal first because it is cheaper, but cracked porcelain cannot be fixed for good, so a crack means a new toilet.

    Yes, our labor is guaranteed. If the install leaks, we come back and fix it free. The toilet itself carries the manufacturer's warranty, usually one to five years on parts and often much longer on the vitreous china, depending on the brand and model. Keep the box label for the model number.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Bigger jobs take a day or more. Wall-hung systems and drain relocations on a slab mean opening walls or floors, so those get scheduled as their own project with time for patches to cure.

    Lower water bills come first. An old 3.5 gallon toilet uses almost three times the water of a 1.28 gallon model. Modern bowls clog less thanks to wider glazed trapways, and the smoother porcelain is easier to keep clean.

    Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. Hard water leaves scale in the rim jets and on the fill valve over time. Weak flushes follow. Look for full glazing and a simple fill valve, and pour vinegar down the overflow tube now and then to keep the jets clear.
