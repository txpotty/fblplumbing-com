    Sewer Relining and Pipe Bursting in Gilroy, CA

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacement in Gilroy

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. It cracks the old pipe outward into the soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. We use it when the old line is too broken or flattened to hold a liner.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Upgrades in Gilroy

    Bursting can upsize the line. A 4-inch clay lateral can be replaced with 6-inch HDPE, which helps on lines that clog because they were undersized from the start or have been added onto with extra bathrooms. Each end only needs a pit around three by four feet. No trench in between.

    Sectional Point Repair in Gilroy

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. It rides in on an inflatable packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. It fixes a single crack or root entry, not a whole failing line. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Commercial Trenchless Sewer Line Work in Gilroy, CA

    Restaurants feel a sewer failure fast, because grease and a dish machine put heavy load on a 4- or 6-inch building drain all day. We can line at night. A trench through a parking lot means lost spaces and a blocked entrance for a week or more. Liners skip that.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Gilroy, CA

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. We wet out a felt tube with epoxy resin, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Ambient cure takes a few hours, and hot water or steam shortens it. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Trenchless Drain Pipe Lining in Gilroy, CA

    Smaller drain lines inside the house can be lined too, including 2-inch kitchen drains and the 3-inch cast iron stacks found in many older homes. We work through cleanouts and roof vents, so the drywall and tile stay where they are. After curing, a small robotic cutter reopens each branch connection from inside the pipe. That saves opening a finished wall.

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow Trenchless Systems in Gilroy

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow both make lining systems we use, each with its own resin chemistry and installation equipment. Both systems are made to meet the ASTM standards written for cured-in-place pipe. The brand matters less than the prep, because a liner bonded to a dirty, scaly pipe wall will not hold the way the manufacturer rates it. So the line gets jetted or descaled to bare wall before any resin goes in.
