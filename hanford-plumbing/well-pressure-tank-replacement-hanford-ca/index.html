    A like-for-like tank replacement often does not require a permit in most counties. Electrical changes or new piping may, and we check local rules for you.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, completely isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    Most new well pressure tanks come with a manufacturer's warranty, typically ranging from 1 to 5 years, covering defects in materials and workmanship. FBL Plumbing also stands behind our labor with our own service guarantee, giving you peace of mind.

    Yes, absolutely. A failing well pressure tank often causes the well pump to short-cycle, meaning it turns on and off too often. This constant starting and stopping puts heavy strain on the pump's motor, leading to premature wear and potential failure of the well pump itself.

    Common signs include your well pump cycling on and off very frequently (short-cycling), fluctuating water pressure at your faucets, no water coming from the tap, or a waterlogged tank when you tap on it. Visible rust or leaks around the tank are also clear indicators.
