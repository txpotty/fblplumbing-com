    Commercial Wall Waterproofing in Hanford

    Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day.     Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Hanford, CA

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the level and wait 24 hours. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a two-piece clamping drain set on the liner. The new pan gets its own flood test before tile.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Hanford

    Copper became standard in many mid-century homes here, and decades later pinhole leaks and corroded fittings are a common repair call. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with real hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and add drain tile wherever water collects at the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Not every basement needs one.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Hanford

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, a membrane over cement board or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Hanford, CA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. It just stops puddling on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Hanford

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Wet winters and long dry summers define the plumbing calendar in Northern California, with drainage problems in the rainy season and outdoor leaks in summer. The joists stop rotting. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Hanford

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Many older homes here sit on raised foundations with crawlspaces, which make supply and drain repairs more accessible than slab work. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Hanford, CA

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. After that the wall gets cleaned, dried and coated with a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Hanford

    Most jobs fall between $3,000 for interior crack sealing and $15,000 or more for exterior excavation with new footing drains. The size of the wall, how wet it gets, the method we pick and how easy the wall is to reach all move the number. Access is a big one. Nothing starts until you have the quote in writing.

    We do. Our workmanship is warrantied, and many membrane systems carry their own manufacturer warranty when they are installed to spec, which is one more reason we never skip a step on cure time or thickness. Your written quote explains the coverage.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.

    Schluter Kerdi, Custom RedGard and Laticrete Hydro Ban cover most of our showers. Sheet membranes like Kerdi suit a fast schedule since there is no cure time, while liquids like RedGard and Hydro Ban go on in two coats and need to dry between them. Each manufacturer's install steps are followed exactly.

    Simple crack injection usually does not need one. Excavation, structural repairs and changes to drainage or the sump discharge often do, and your local building department decides which jobs need one, so we check before we quote and file the paperwork if one is needed. We list any permit fee on the quote.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Bigger foundation jobs take several days, sometimes more if it rains.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide because soil movement, the water table and the quality of the original install all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Drain tile can silt up over time.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. The sump pump will need replacing well before that. Most pumps last seven to ten years.
