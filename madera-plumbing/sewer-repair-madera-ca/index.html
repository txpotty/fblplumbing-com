    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Madera

    A single spot repair usually runs $1,500 to $4,500 depending on depth and access. Trenchless lining usually costs $80 to $250 per foot, and full replacement of a typical residential line ranges from $5,000 to $15,000. We give you a written quote after the camera inspection is done.

    Most spot repairs are completed in one day, including excavation and backfill. Larger replacements or jobs that need a city right-of-way permit can take two to four days. We give you a timeline before we start.

    We use Schedule 40 PVC or ABS that meets local code, joined with approved fittings and shielded no-hub couplings at transitions. For lining, we use epoxy-saturated liners rated for decades of service.

    A small rental auger can clear a soft clog, but it will not fix a crack, belly or root intrusion. Forcing a cable through a broken pipe can make the damage worse. A camera inspection tells you whether you have a clog or a structural problem.

    Trenchless sewer repair is a method of repairing or replacing sewer lines with minimal digging, often requiring only one or two access points. It involves techniques like pipe bursting, where a new pipe is pulled through the old one, or pipe lining, where a resin-impregnated liner is inserted and cured inside the existing pipe. This preserves your landscaping and is less disruptive than traditional methods.

    The duration of a sewer repair depends heavily on the scope of work. Simple spot repairs or hydro-jetting might take a few hours. Trenchless repairs like pipe lining can usually be completed in one to two days. Full sewer line replacements involving excavation can take anywhere from two to five days, depending on the length and complexity of the line.

    To prevent future sewer line problems, avoid flushing anything other than toilet paper, dispose of grease properly and be mindful of what goes down your drains. Consider having annual professional drain cleaning or sewer inspections, especially if you have older pipes or mature trees near your sewer line. Regular maintenance can catch small issues before they become major problems.

    For sewer line replacements, we mainly use durable, code-compliant materials such as PVC for its longevity and resistance to corrosion, or HDPE for trenchless applications. We select the best material based on your needs and local soil conditions.
