    What Our Tankless Plumbers Fix in Madera, CA

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Madera, CA

    Noritz heaters hold up well, though flow sensors and vent joints still fail with age. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. We clean the fan and burner, check the vent joints for gaps, and test the flow sensor's output against the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.

    Tankless Descaling and Flushing in Madera

    Rinnai shows an LC code and Navien posts a scale warning when the exchanger runs hot from mineral buildup. Clear the code without flushing and it comes right back, because the scale is still in there. We connect a circulation pump to the service valves, circulate descaling solution through the exchanger for close to an hour, then flush and clean the inlet screen. Yearly is right for most hard-water homes.

    Tankless Repair vs Replacement Cost in Madera, CA

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350, and sensor or igniter repairs land between $250 and $600. A heat exchanger out of warranty can cost $800 to $1,500 in parts and labor. Replacement is a bigger number. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Madera, CA

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, so the unit lights at low flow and fails when two showers run.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Madera

    Navien condensing units run a second stainless exchanger that pulls heat from the exhaust. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. On NPE-A models we also test the built-in recirculation pump, after clearing the trap and checking vent pitch. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Madera

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, and they hold temperature off thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. We pull the screen, read sensor values in the service menu and compare thermistor resistance to the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Madera, CA

    Rinnai units are common, and most of their trouble starts with scale. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and each part gets tested against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Madera

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just at the moment it lights. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, since pressure that sags under load leads to flame loss and slugs of cold water. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Madera

    Minor fixes like a flame rod or a flow sensor tend to land between $150 and $350. A heat exchanger or control board out of warranty can push a repair past $800, so we quote a new unit next to it for comparison. You see the number first.

    We service Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Bosch and AO Smith gas units, along with electric models from Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart. Odd or discontinued brands can be harder to source parts for.

    Plan on one to three hours for a common fix like a flame rod or flow sensor. If the part is not on the truck, we usually return within a few business days.

    Most take one to three hours on site. A descale by itself runs about ninety minutes, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. We tell you which before we leave.

    Codes fire when a sensor reads something outside its range: low flow, no flame signal, high exhaust temperature or a blocked vent. Every code has its own test.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and those passages are small. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, and that can turn a routine descale into an exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen two or three times a year. Putting a softener or scale cartridge upstream cuts scale buildup a great deal, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. Next on the list are blocked air intakes, dirty flame rods and failed flow sensors, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of that list can be prevented.
