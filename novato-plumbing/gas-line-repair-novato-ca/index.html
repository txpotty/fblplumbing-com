    Propane Gas Line Service in Novato, CA

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. We repair buried and above-ground propane lines, replace leaking regulators and fittings and check that each appliance has been converted with the correct orifices for propane. Wrong orifices make a burner run dirty and unsafe. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.

    Pool Heater, Fire Pit and Grill Lines in Novato, CA

    Backyard gas lines for pool heaters, fire pits and built-in grills face sun, weather and irrigation water that shorten their life. A pool heater can pull 250,000 BTU or more, which can rob gas from the furnace and water heater if the line was sized for a grill. Sizing is the first thing we check. New and repaired lines get tested, buried to the required depth and laid with tracer wire.

    Black Iron Pipe Repair in Novato, CA

    Black iron is the standard interior gas pipe in a lot of homes, and it lasts a long time indoors. Joints are the weak point. Years of vibration from a furnace or a house settling can loosen threaded fittings, and pipe in a damp garage or an exterior wall can rust from the outside in. Bad sections get replaced with new pipe. Rusted pipe that runs outside gets painted or replaced with coated steel so it does not come back as the same problem.

    Gas Leak Repair at Fittings and Valves in Novato

    Most residential gas leaks are at a threaded joint, a valve stem or a flexible connector, not in the middle of a pipe. That keeps repairs small. Leaking joints get taken apart, cleaned, resealed with gas-rated compound and tightened, and damaged fittings or valves get replaced. Every repair gets a soap test and a system pressure test before we leave.

    Gas Line Relocation and Upgrades in Novato, CA

    A kitchen remodel or a new tankless water heater can push a gas system past what its original pipe can deliver. Tankless units need a lot of gas. Many tankless heaters are rated at 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which can be more than double what an old tank heater used, so the line feeding it often has to be upsized back toward the meter. We calculate the full load first. The new pipe gets sized so every burner in the house still has full pressure at once.
