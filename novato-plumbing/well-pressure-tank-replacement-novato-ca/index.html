    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Novato

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the usable water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the most effective ways to extend pump life.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Novato

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and restrict flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives accurate readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in reliability.

    Common signs include your well pump constantly turning on and off (short cycling), fluctuating water pressure, air sputtering from faucets, or a tank that is waterlogged (feels heavy when tapped and full of water). If you notice any of these, it is time for an inspection.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, completely isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, well pressure tank replacement involves working with pressurized water, electrical components, and ensuring proper sizing and calibration. It is highly recommended to hire a professional plumber to ensure safety, code compliance, and proper system function.

    Most pressure tank replacements take two to four hours. Adding a constant pressure system or multiple tanks can take the better part of a day.

    On average, a well pressure tank can last between 5 to 10 years, though some high-quality tanks can last longer. Factors like water quality, proper initial installation, and regular maintenance can greatly impact its lifespan.

    If your switch is more than a few years old or the contacts are pitted, replacing it with the tank is low-cost insurance. It also lets us match the switch settings to the new tank.
