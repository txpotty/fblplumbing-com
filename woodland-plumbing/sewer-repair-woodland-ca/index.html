    Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Woodland, CA

    Trenchless repair means we fix the pipe without opening a trench along its full length. Usually two small pits. Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE line through the old one and splits the old pipe outward, while cured-in-place lining pushes a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe and hardens it into a new wall. Both save driveways and mature trees. Not every line qualifies, and we will say so.

    Sewer Line Hydro-Jetting in Woodland

    A hydro-jetter pushes water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scours the full inside of the pipe. Snaking just opens a path. That makes jetting the better choice for grease, scale and fine root growth, and it is the standard prep step before any pipe lining job. Brittle or broken pipe is a different story. We camera first to make sure the pipe can take the pressure.

    Cracked and Broken Pipe Repair in Woodland

    Clay and cast iron crack from settling soil, tree roots and vehicles parked over the line. Dirt falls in through the crack. We excavate at the marked spot, cut out the damaged length and install new Schedule 40 PVC with shielded stainless couplings rated for the transition between materials, then test the repair with water and video before closing the hole. Most of these are one-day jobs.

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Woodland, CA

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Then the sewer smell shows up. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, so we check the vent any time the drains act up but the main line looks clean. Fixes range from a quick clearing to a new section of stack.

    Commercial Sewer Repair in Woodland

    Multifamily buildings and offices put several units' worth of flow through one lateral. Small defects turn into big backups. Night and weekend work is available.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Woodland, CA

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Paper and solids settle there. Many older homes here sit on raised foundations with crawlspaces, which make supply and drain repairs easier to reach than slab work. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and reset the slope to the quarter inch per foot most codes call for on residential laterals. Not every sag needs digging, and we will tell you which yours is.

    Tree Root Invasion Solutions in Woodland

    Roots in a sewer line are not the tree's fault. A leaking joint invited them in. Warm, wet, nutrient-rich air escaping from a loose joint draws roots toward the line, and once they get in they keep growing until the joint splits or the pipe cracks open. We cut them out, then talk about the long-term fix. A liner or new pipe at that joint is what actually stops regrowth.
