    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in San Ramon

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in San Ramon. The low end covers a pump swap in an existing pit, while a new pit cut through the slab, a long discharge run, or a battery backup pushes the total toward the top of that range. We quote after seeing it.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Most local codes do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can draw a fine.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. You are dealing with wiring, pipe and concrete in one job, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. We pick the model by horsepower and lift, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your San Ramon basement actually takes on. We default to cast iron.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. Parts for all four are easy to find at supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. We stock the common models.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. New pits, new outlets and discharge routing are a different story, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in San Ramon. Those parts call for a licensed plumber.
