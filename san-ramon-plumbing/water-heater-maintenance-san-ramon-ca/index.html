    Tank Water Heater Maintenance in San Ramon

    Flushing is the heart of tank maintenance. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. The tank gets drained until it runs clear, then refilled, and the anode, relief valve and connections get checked. Takes about an hour. A clean tank heats faster and lasts longer.

    Electric Water Heater Tune-Up in San Ramon

    Electric tanks fail at the lower element. Sediment buries the lower element until it overheats and fails, leaving you with a short supply of hot water. We test both elements. With power off, we check thermostats and connections.

    Gas Water Heater Safety Checks in San Ramon, CA

    Gas units need a clean burner and a good draft. Leak checks, flame inspection, burner cleaning and a draft test are part of every gas visit. Carbon monoxide is the concern. The thermocouple or flame sensor gets checked as well.

    Electric Element Service in San Ramon, CA

    Two elements, two thermostats. Sediment coats the lower element first, it runs hot and burns out, and that is the most common reason an electric tank suddenly gives you only half a tank of hot water. We test with power off. Good parts stay in.

    Thermostat, Element and Burner Checks in San Ramon

    Wrong temperature means a part is off. On electric units we test the thermostats and elements for proper resistance and accurate shutoff, and on gas units we inspect the burner, the pilot or igniter and the gas control valve. Coastal fog and moisture can speed corrosion on exposed fittings, so exterior valves and hose bibs deserve an occasional check. Faulty parts waste energy. You run out sooner.

    Descaling Tankless Units in San Ramon

    Error codes often mean scale. Scale in the exchanger cuts flow and trips the unit's safety shutoff. A pump kit handles it. Yearly descaling is standard advice for hard water.

    Anode Rod Checks in San Ramon

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. Once the rod is eaten down to its core wire, the water goes after the tank itself. We pull it and look. Replacing the rod is much cheaper than replacing the heater.
