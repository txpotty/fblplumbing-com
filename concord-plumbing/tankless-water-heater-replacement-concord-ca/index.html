    Condensing Tankless Replacement in Concord

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE series or Rinnai Sensei reach efficiencies above 90 percent by capturing heat from exhaust gases. They vent through inexpensive PVC and produce acidic condensate that must be neutralized and drained properly. We install a neutralizer cartridge and route the condensate line to an approved drain. Inland valleys get hot summers that work water heaters and exterior lines hard, while coastal areas stay mild and damp year round. Condensing units still save gas because they work year-round on showers, laundry and dishes. We explain the cost difference so you can decide if the efficiency gain is worth the price.

    Frequent error codes, fluctuating temperature, water leaking from inside the cabinet and a unit older than 15 years are common signs. If the heat exchanger is leaking, replacement is usually the better value.

    DIY tankless water heater replacement is generally not recommended due to the complexity of gas lines, high-voltage electricity, water connections and venting requirements. Improper installation can lead to dangerous gas leaks, carbon monoxide poisoning, water damage or electrical hazards. It is best to hire a licensed and experienced plumber like FBL Plumbing.

    A standard tankless water heater replacement, if you are upgrading from an existing tankless unit, usually takes 4 to 8 hours. If you are converting from a traditional tank water heater, the process can take longer, possibly a full day, due to additional plumbing, gas or electrical changes required for proper installation and venting.

They only heat water on demand, eliminating the standby heat loss that comes with keeping a large tank of water hot. This can lead to real savings on your utility bills over time, particularly in homes with varying hot water usage.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a new tankless water heater typically lasts 20 to 25 years. This is significantly longer than traditional tank water heaters, which usually last 10 to 15 years. Regular flushing and descaling can help extend the life of your tankless unit, especially in areas with hard water.
