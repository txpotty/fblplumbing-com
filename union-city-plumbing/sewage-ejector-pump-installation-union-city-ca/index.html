    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is built specifically to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve separate purposes.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump usually has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help extend its operating life.

    New ejector installations typically require a plumbing permit and inspection. Straight pump replacements may not, and we confirm local requirements for each job.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that requires immediate attention.

    FBL Plumbing offers a comprehensive warranty on all sewage ejector pump installations, covering both parts and labor. The warranty period can vary based on the pump manufacturer's warranty and our service guarantee. We will provide full details on your coverage before any work begins.

    A sump pump removes groundwater or seepage, while an ejector pump handles sewage and wastewater from fixtures. Ejector pumps are designed to pass solids and require a sealed, vented basin.
