    Permits for Polybutylene Replacement in Napa

    A whole-house poly replacement in Napa is a repipe, and it usually needs a plumbing permit. The permit is on us.     Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Napa

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Check the heater first. It also shows up under sinks, at the main and through garage walls, and we follow it into the attic to see how much of the house it feeds.

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Napa

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. On a slab home, we route the new lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, which keeps every supply line out of the concrete. PEX handles a freeze better than copper. We use expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter open, so pressure at the far shower stays strong.

    Polybutylene Leak Repair in Napa

    Repairs on polybutylene are temporary by nature, because the pipe next to the repair is the same age. A repair buys time. We cut out the failed piece and splice in PEX with listed fittings, stopping the leak and giving you time to plan the full job.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Napa

    Copper is a solid alternative to PEX for poly replacement. It is rigid, handles heat well and has decades of history in homes. Plan on a higher price. Some water can pinhole copper over time, so we look at your water and any past leak history before recommending it over PEX.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Napa

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper runs higher, and a yard line usually adds $1,500 to $4,000.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

    Some disruption is unavoidable, but most families stay home the whole time. Expect daytime shutoffs. We cover floors and furniture, clean up every evening and turn the water back on before we leave, so you can still shower, cook and run the dishwasher at night.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Larger homes take longer. The schedule depends on the number of fixtures, attic access and whether you choose PEX or copper, and water is usually back on at the end of each work day.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Terms are on your estimate. PEX-A jobs done by certified installers also get the maker's long-term warranty, which we register on your behalf.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Leaks at fittings are another. Since poly can look normal right before it splits, a home from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe should plan a replacement even before any leaks.
