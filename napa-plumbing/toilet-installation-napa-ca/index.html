    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Napa

    Most homeowners in Napa, CA pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. A stubborn old toilet adds time. Rusted bolts get cut off, and a cast iron flange that cracks on the way out needs a repair ring, both adding labor.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Then pick round or elongated. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    Just about any brand you can buy at a supply house or home center. TOTO and American Standard are the ones we set most often. Gerber and Mansfield are common in rentals because parts are cheap and easy to get, and we can usually tell you which one fits your budget and your rough-in after a short call. Bring us the model number.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. It costs more and weighs more. A two-piece bolts the tank to the bowl with a rubber gasket, which makes it lighter to carry up stairs and cheaper to fix, since you can replace just the tank or just the bowl if one cracks. Both flush well.

    All the major ones, including Kohler, TOTO, American Standard and Gerber. Ask us before you buy if you are unsure. We know which models flush well and which use odd parts that get hard to find once a flapper wears out.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. Swapping in the same place usually does not. The permit covers drain sizing and venting, and the inspector wants to see both before the floor or wall is closed, so we schedule the inspection around your remodel timeline.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. Some older homes and tight bathrooms have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet built for the wrong distance either will not fit against the wall or leaves an awkward gap behind the tank. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.
