    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Napa, CA

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. A different rod usually solves it. We put in an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Napa, CA

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Sediment Flushing in Napa, CA

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. We flush with full line pressure, opening and closing the cold supply in bursts to stir the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Napa, CA

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. If six inches of wire is bare, it is time for a new one.

    Commercial Tank Maintenance in Napa, CA

    Restaurants, salons, laundromats and small offices run tanks much harder than a household does, often 75 to 100 gallons cycling all day. Scale piles up much faster than at home. Most commercial tanks go on a quarterly or twice-yearly schedule with a dated log on the jacket. Downtime gets scheduled outside business hours.

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Napa

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. Figure on half an hour or a bit more for the drain. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Small leaks at the dielectric nipples get noted on your report.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Napa

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. That is what makes the popping noise. For stubborn scale we use a potable-rated descaler, then flush it clean. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Napa, CA

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. You get a straight answer on which one is worth it. Past 10 to 12 years, rust at the base or a seam leak means replacement. Younger tanks with a sound liner are worth maintaining.
