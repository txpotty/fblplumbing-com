    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Napa, CA

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. It was installed in a lot of homes in the decades after the war, and over time it softens, blisters and squashes into an oval that catches everything going by. Clay holds up better, except at the joints. Several bad spots on one run usually make full replacement the better buy.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Napa

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Roots get into clay joints, cast iron rusts out along the invert, and Orangeburg fiber pipe squashes flat over the years. Modern plastic pipe avoids all three problems. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Napa

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. The crew trenches the full length of the lateral, removes the failed pipe and sets new PVC on compacted bedding with steady fall. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Napa

    No replacement quote without video. We run a self-leveling color camera from the cleanout to the main and record every joint, crack, root mass and low spot along the way, with footage you keep. A sonde in the camera head lets us mark the depth on the lawn. The footage tells us whether one section or the whole line needs to go.

    Keeping a New Sewer Line Healthy in Napa

    New pipe still needs a little care. Keep willows, silver maples and other thirsty trees well away from the sewer path, because even a good line can get stressed by a large root system pushing on it. Earthquakes and hillside soil movement are real risks in Northern California, so flexible connections and seismic straps help protect plumbing from sudden movement. Wipes do not break down. A camera check every few years on an older house catches problems at the city tap or the cleanout early.
