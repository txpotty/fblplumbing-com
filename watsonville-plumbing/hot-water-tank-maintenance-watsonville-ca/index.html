    What a Tank Tune-Up Covers in Watsonville, CA

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Watsonville, CA

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. Swapping the rod usually fixes it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Relief Valve Testing in Watsonville

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. If it keeps dripping, we replace it. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Watsonville, CA

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. A tired thermocouple drops the pilot, so we test it too. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Watsonville, CA

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. Electric units get a meter test on both elements and a look at the wiring at each thermostat, where loose terminals can scorch and melt the insulation. Both get a flush.

    Standard Tank Flushing in Watsonville

    Flushing is the one job every tank needs, gas or electric, new or old. Hard water makes it more urgent. We drain the tank completely, refill it partway, drain it again and repeat until the hose runs clear, which on a heavily scaled tank can take four or five cycles. Then we close the valve and check that it reseals without a drip.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Watsonville

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib. Then we lift the relief valve lever. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Watsonville, CA

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Three to five years is typical. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.

    Sediment Flushing in Watsonville, CA

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. We flush with full line pressure, opening and closing the cold supply in bursts to stir the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.
