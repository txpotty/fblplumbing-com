    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Watsonville

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Repair or Replace: Straight Answers in Watsonville, CA

    Before any wrench turns, you get a written price for the repair and an honest read on the tank in your Watsonville, CA home.     Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Watsonville

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Every suspect part then gets tested with a meter.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Watsonville, CA

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Watsonville, CA

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Watsonville

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.
