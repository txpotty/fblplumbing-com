    Expansion Tank Installation in Davis, CA

    Many homes have a pressure reducing valve or check valve that creates a closed system. When water heats and expands, pressure can rise and stress pipes and relief valves. An expansion tank absorbs that extra pressure. We install and pre-charge the tank to match your water pressure. This protects your new water heater and pipes.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Davis

    The cost of water heater installation in Davis, CA can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units usually have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your particular needs.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by flow rate.

    A traditional tank water heater usually lasts between 8 and 12 years with proper maintenance. Tankless water heaters often have a longer lifespan, ranging from 15 to 20 years, thanks to their durable components and on-demand operation.

    Rusty water, leaks around the base, rumbling noises and running out of hot water quickly are common signs. If your heater is over 10 years old, replacement is often wise.

    New water heaters typically come with a manufacturer's warranty on the tank and components, usually ranging from 6 to 12 years. FBL Plumbing also provides a labor warranty on our installation work, giving you peace of mind. We will discuss exact warranty details for your chosen unit during the consultation.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for water heater replacement. We pull permits when required and make sure the install meets code.

    The correct size water heater depends on the number of people in your household and your peak hot water demand. For tank units, a 40 to 50 gallon tank is common for 2 to 4 people, while larger families might need 60 to 80 gallons. For tankless units, it is about flow rate (GPM) and temperature rise. Our plumbers will assess your needs to recommend the right size.

    If your home has a pressure reducing valve or check valve, an expansion tank is typically required. It protects your water heater and pipes from pressure spikes.
