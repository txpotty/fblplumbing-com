    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Davis, CA

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. It is also the stretch that takes the most weather, sitting in the sun or the soil. Risers rust at grade. A new riser or a new buried run gets tested and left ready for the utility to turn on. Expect a half day for most riser swap.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Davis, CA

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. Bonding means a 6 AWG copper wire from the gas piping to the electrical grounding system. Plenty of older installs lack it. Repairs use the same brand's fittings as the existing tubing, since manufacturers do not allow mixing parts. A splice that skips that rule fails the inspection.

    Gas Line Repair and Replacement in Davis, CA

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. The joint gets broken, cleaned and remade with new pipe dope rated for gas. Cracked fittings get replaced. Badly rusted sections, often near a crawlspace vent, are cut back to clean pipe and replaced. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a pressure test.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Davis

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. Regulator vents and lockup pressure get checked along with every joint. Rooftop units leak too. If a shutdown would cost you a dinner rush, we valve off just the bad branch and leave the rest live. Staff get a short briefing on the shutoff locations.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Davis, CA

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. We swap in a new coated stainless connector, add a quarter-turn ball valve and set a sediment trap where the code calls for it. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is relit.
