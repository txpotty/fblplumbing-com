    Main Line and Sewer Clearing in Davis, CA

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Davis

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Both have weak spots. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Jetting a Sewer Line in Davis, CA

    Grease and scale are jetting work. Cables leave them behind. A jetter nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself down the line and blasts the buildup off the pipe wall, restoring close to the full diameter of the pipe and slowing the next clog.

    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Davis, CA

    Grease hardens as it cools. It shrinks the opening a little every week. A greasy main slowly loses diameter until it cannot carry a full washer load or a bathtub draining at once, and jetting is the right fix.

    Scheduled Sewer Cleaning in Davis, CA

    History predicts the next backup. We set a cleaning interval. A line that has backed up before can be cleaned on a set schedule, often every year or two, so it never stops again without warning.

    Tree Root Removal in Davis, CA Sewers

    Roots get in through tiny gaps. Once inside, they grow fast. A root saw on a sectional cable cuts the mass loose so we can pull it out through the cleanout, and a jetter can follow to flush out the fine root hairs left on the pipe wall, and a camera shows where they are getting in.

    Sewer Cleaning Versus Sewer Repair in Davis, CA

    Cleaning cannot fix a broken pipe. It only removes what is inside. If your sewer backs up every few months, the camera usually shows a belly, a crack or a collapsed section, and we compare the yearly cost of cleanings to a spot repair, a liner or a replacement, with the numbers in writing.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Davis, CA

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be slightly corrosive to older copper pipe. Flow slows a little each year. Cabling opens a path, while jetting strips the crust off the walls so the line stays open longer, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.

    Several drains backing up at once is a main line problem. Every drain shares that one pipe. After the line is open, a camera run tells us if the blockage was debris or something structural that will keep catching waste.
