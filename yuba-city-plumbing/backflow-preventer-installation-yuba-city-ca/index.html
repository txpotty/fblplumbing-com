    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Yuba City

    Residential pressure vacuum breakers usually cost $250 to $700 installed. Double check and RPZ assemblies for larger homes or businesses range from $800 to $3,500 or more depending on size and enclosure needs.

    We install all common types of backflow preventers, including reduced pressure zone assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and double check valve assemblies. Our team will recommend the appropriate device based on your property's hazard level and local plumbing codes in Yuba City.

    We install Watts, Febco, Wilkins by Zurn and Apollo assemblies. Each has readily available repair kits, which keeps future service affordable.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This verifies the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also assist with scheduling these annual tests.

    Backflow preventers are usually installed as a preventive measure or because code requires them. Signs of possible cross-contamination include discolored or foul-smelling tap water, unexplained drops in water pressure or visible dirty water entering a clean water source, though these often point to broader plumbing issues as well.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, especially if piping must be reconfigured.

    In Yuba City, most backflow preventers must be tested once a year by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is crucial for ongoing compliance and safety.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice comes down to the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.
