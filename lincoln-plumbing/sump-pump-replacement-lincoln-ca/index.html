    Wayne Sump Pump Replacement in Lincoln

    Wayne makes everything from a thermoplastic quarter-horse pump at the hardware store to cast iron models with vertical switches, and the cheap ones are what we replace most.     Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Lincoln

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. Pumps and oil-sensing panels get replaced to code. Two alternating pumps on one controller mean one can fail without flooding the pit. Some insurance carriers ask for test records.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Replacement in Lincoln, CA

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the pit, out of the water, which is why a good one can run fifteen years in a dry basement. They are louder, no question. We set the new column plumb, secure it so vibration does not walk it across the basin, and adjust the float rod stops for the cycle you want. Narrow pits, under about 12 inches across, are where they make the most sense.

    Backup Pump Options in Lincoln, CA

    Tree roots seek out sewer laterals during dry spells, which is why heavy rains often reveal cracked lines through sudden backups. Outages tend to hit right when the rain is heaviest and the pit is filling fastest. The backup kicks on if the primary dies or the power goes out. Where water pressure is strong, a water-powered backup runs off the supply line with no battery at all, protected by a backflow preventer. Wells cannot run them.

    Cast Iron Submersible Pumps in Lincoln

    Cast iron sheds motor heat into the water around it far better than a plastic housing does, which is a big part of why a Zoeller M53 or Liberty 250 outlasts a hardware-store pump.     Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Lincoln

    Most replacements in Lincoln land between $500 and $1,500, and the spread comes down to the pump you choose and whether the pit needs work. Plan on another $300 to $800 for a battery backup. We put the number in writing first.

    A battery backup is a second pump, usually 12-volt, that sits slightly above the primary on its own float and runs off a deep-cycle battery. If the main pump dies or the power drops, it takes over. The charger keeps it ready between storms.

    Seven to ten years is typical. Pumps that sit idle for long stretches can fail sooner because the switch sticks.

    Zoeller, Liberty Pumps, Wayne and Little Giant cover most of what we put in. For a primary pump we usually recommend cast iron. We can usually install a brand you prefer, provided it is rated for your discharge height and basin.

    One to two hours for a straight swap. Adding a battery backup or a new discharge line takes longer.

    Like-for-like pump swaps usually do not need a permit in most jurisdictions. New wiring or a rerouted discharge line may trigger one, and the rules change quite a bit from one city to the next. We look it up before work begins.

    Every three months, and right before the wet season. Fill the pit with a bucket until the float lifts. You want it to kick on, drop the water to the off point and shut down cleanly, with no hum or rattle.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. The backup, battery or water powered, sits a few inches higher and only runs if the primary fails, loses power or cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. Most basements are better off with one.
