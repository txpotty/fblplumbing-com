    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Lincoln

    A single clogged sink or tub in Lincoln, CA usually runs $200 to $350. Main sewer lines with roots or jetting tend to land between $400 and $1,000. Location changes the price. An easy outdoor cleanout keeps costs lower, and pulling a toilet or going through a roof vent adds labor. You get the number before we start.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting works another way. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Lincoln's older homes suffer the worst of it. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    No permit is needed for routine rooting. A new cleanout or a pipe repair may need one. We pull permits for any repair work we do.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    It can be, so we prepare for it. We lay cloths from the entry to the drain and keep buckets and rags at the cleanout. Cables come out wet. We wipe it as it retracts and bag any debris before it leaves the area, then clean up before we go.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water rising in a tub or shower when another fixture drains is a strong sign of a main line clog. That one is urgent. Waiting raises the odds that the next flush puts sewage on the floor.
