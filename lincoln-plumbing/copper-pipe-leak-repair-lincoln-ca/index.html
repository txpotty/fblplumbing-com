    PEX resists the corrosion and scale that cause copper pinholes and is flexible and quick to install. If you have repeated copper leaks, repiping with PEX is usually a cost-effective long-term solution.

    Type K, L and M copper pipes differ primarily in their wall thickness and thus their pressure ratings. Type K has the thickest walls and is used for underground service and main water lines. Type L is common for interior water supply and radiant heating. Type M has the thinnest walls and is usually used for less demanding applications. We are skilled in repairing all types.

    Most accessible repairs take one to two hours. Leaks inside walls or ceilings can take two to four hours, and slab leak repairs may take a whole day.

    Common causes include aggressive water chemistry, high water velocity, excessive pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues. These factors erode the pipe wall from the inside over time.

    To prevent future copper pipe leaks, consider installing a whole-house water softener if you have hard water, as mineral buildup can lead to corrosion. Maintaining appropriate water pressure, insulating pipes for cold weather and scheduling periodic plumbing inspections can also greatly extend the life of your copper pipes.

    Copper pipes can leak due to several factors, including corrosion from water chemistry when the pH is too acidic or alkaline, aggressive soil conditions, high water pressure, poor installation or physical damage from foundation shifts. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be slightly corrosive to older copper pipe. Pinhole leaks usually result from long-term internal corrosion.
