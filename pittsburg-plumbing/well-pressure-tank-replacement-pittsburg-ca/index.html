    Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Pittsburg

    Leaks at the tank tee, a gauge or a fitting can be fixed. A leak from the tank itself cannot. Bladder tanks with a fixed bladder cannot be rebuilt, so a torn bladder or rusted seam means a new tank, while a leaking tee or relief valve is a simple part swap we can usually do in under an hour.

    Well-X-Trol Pressure Tank Installation in Pittsburg, CA

    Amtrol's Well-X-Trol line uses a diaphragm design. We put in plenty of them. Its stainless or polypropylene water connection keeps water off the steel shell, limiting internal corrosion, and the factory pre-charge still gets checked and adjusted to your switch on install.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Pittsburg, CA

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. Worn contacts can fail to open or close. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, since a new tank paired with a tired switch is asking for trouble. Cheap part.

    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Pittsburg, CA

    Constant pressure systems use a variable speed drive that adjusts pump speed to demand. Pressure stays steady even with two showers running. These systems can run on a smaller tank, though the drive and wiring add to the price. We explain the trade-off.

    Same-Day Tank Swaps in Pittsburg

    Our vans carry the sizes most homes use. That means a call in the morning can often end with a new tank installed by afternoon, including new tank tee, gauge and relief valve, because the parts are already with us instead of on a supply house shelf. Bigger tanks may take a day to order.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Pittsburg, CA

    Diaphragm tanks use a flexible sheet clamped inside the shell. Air sits above it, water below. When it fails, symptoms match a bladder tank, including fast cycling and water at the Schrader valve. Replacement is the usual answer.

    Preventative Well Pressure Tank Maintenance in Pittsburg, CA

    Check the air charge once a year. Shut off the pump, drain the system to zero, and put a tire gauge on the valve at the top of the tank, then add air with a small compressor if it has dropped. Water out of the valve means a torn bladder.

    Whole-House Tank Replacement on Older Wells in Pittsburg

    Older wells sometimes have a tank, switch and plumbing that were all installed together decades ago. A new tank can reveal tired fittings. We look at the whole tank area, from the check valve to the house shutoff, so you can choose a tank-only swap or a fuller refresh. Your call.
