    Perma-Liner Trenchless Solutions in Pittsburg

    Perma-Liner makes inversion drums and epoxy systems for lining 2- to 6-inch drain lines, which covers most of what we see in houses.     Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Pittsburg

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. It goes oval and blisters, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Vitrified clay that still holds shape lines well.

    Emergency Trenchless Sewer Repairs in Pittsburg

    Sewage coming up through a floor drain is an emergency, but the first fix is usually a cable or a jetter to get the line flowing again. Lining comes after that. FBL Plumbing can often schedule the liner within a few days. In the meantime the house drains normally.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Upgrades in Pittsburg

    Bursting can upsize the line. A 4-inch clay lateral can be replaced with 6-inch HDPE, which helps on lines that clog because they were undersized from the start or have been added onto with extra bathrooms. The pit at each end is small. No trench in between.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Pittsburg, CA

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, that beats jackhammering.

    Pipe Bursting in Pittsburg, CA

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Pittsburg, CA

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. The crew saturates a felt tube with two-part epoxy on a roller table at the truck, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Cure time runs a few hours. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.
