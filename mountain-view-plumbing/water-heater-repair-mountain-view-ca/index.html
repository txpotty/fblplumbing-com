    A typical repair costs $150 to $600, while a new standard tank installed often runs $1,400 to $2,800 depending on size, fuel and code updates.     Leaking Water Heater Repair in Mountain View, CA

    Not every leak means a dead tank. Relief valve, drain valve and supply line drips are cheap fixes. Water from the tank body or bottom seam usually means the lining has failed, and replacement is the only real fix. We protect your floors either way.

    Gas Water Heater Repair in Mountain View, CA

    Gas units add a burner, a pilot or igniter and a gas control valve to the list of parts that can fail. Most no-heat calls trace to the thermocouple or the gas control. After any repair, every gas connection gets a leak test. Draft at the vent gets checked too.

    Tankless Water Heater Repair in Mountain View

    Scale is the main enemy of a tankless heater. It coats the heat exchanger and trips the unit into an error code. A descaling flush, a clean inlet screen and a check of the igniter and flow sensor cover most tankless calls. All major brands.

    Pilot Repair in Mountain View

    A pilot that lights and then dies when you let go of the knob almost always means a weak thermocouple. Less often it is a dirty flame arrestor screen choking off combustion air. We test the thermocouple output in millivolts. A good reading points us to the gas valve instead.

    Electric Water Heater Repair in Mountain View

    Two elements and two thermostats run a standard electric tank, and any one of the four can quit without warning. If the water is warm but runs out quickly, the lower element is the first suspect. We test resistance with a meter. The replacement parts ride on the truck, which keeps it to one visit.

    Water Heater Maintenance and Tune-Ups in Mountain View, CA

    A yearly tune-up adds years to a tank. The visit covers a sediment flush, an anode check, a relief valve test and a look at the connections. Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running hard. Left alone, the sediment hardens until a hose flush cannot break it loose.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Mountain View, CA

    The anode rod sacrifices itself so the steel tank does not rust. In hard water it can be eaten away within a few years. After pulling the old rod, we fit a magnesium, aluminum or powered anode to suit your water and any smell. Rotten-egg odor often clears up.
