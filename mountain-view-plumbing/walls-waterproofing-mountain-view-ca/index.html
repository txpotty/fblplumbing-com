    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Mountain View

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane foam, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Mountain View, CA

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Wet winters and long dry summers define the plumbing calendar in Northern California, with drainage problems in the rainy season and irrigation leaks in summer. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. Most homeowners notice the musty smell is gone within a few weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Mountain View

    Older neighborhoods in Northern California include prewar and postwar homes with galvanized supply lines and cast iron or clay sewer pipe that are nearing the end of their life. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with steady hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Not every basement needs one.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Mountain View

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Mountain View take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days.     Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Mountain View, CA

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. Without one, the discharge water runs back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.
