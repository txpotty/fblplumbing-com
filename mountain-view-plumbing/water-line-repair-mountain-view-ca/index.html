    Copper Water Line Repair in Mountain View

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Pinholes show up where stones press on the pipe, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. We usually cut out two feet. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, and we check the exposed pipe on both sides for green crust or soft spots.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Mountain View

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer homes across Northern California mostly use PEX or copper with plastic drains, and many owners are repiping older houses to restore water pressure. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. We price each one for you.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Mountain View, CA

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. We shut it down first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Mountain View

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Mountain View

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the main shutoff and the curb valve for anything half closed, and look at the pipe itself. Old galvanized can be choked to half its size with rust. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Mountain View, CA

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Two or more leaks on galvanized is a replacement. Age matters too, since most service lines past 40 years old are living on borrowed time. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Mountain View

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, then swap a failed regulator for a new Watts or Zurn Wilkins valve when it reads too high. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. So the shutoff works when you need it.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Mountain View, CA

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and sunlight exposure before burial weakens the plastic over time. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. A shallow spot repair is cheap.     Green grass in one strip is a big one. Soggy ground in dry weather is another. You may also notice pressure dropping at every fixture, a rising water bill, cloudy water or a hiss near the meter. If you see two or more of these together, it is worth a meter test.

    Often the pipe itself is not covered. Damage it causes may be. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their insurer or the water provider, which can pay for the repair or replacement of the buried line itself. We provide photos and an itemized invoice for your claim.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. All three work well. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX tolerates ground movement and freezing, and HDPE is the standard choice when we pull a line trenchless. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.
