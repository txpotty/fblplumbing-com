    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Mountain View

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    Emergency PEX Pipe Repair & Installation in Mountain View, CA

    PEX rarely fails on its own. Most failures trace back to a nail or screw through the pipe, a rodent working on an attic run, or a fitting that was not crimped fully. We carry the common sizes. Most emergency PEX repairs in Mountain View, CA come down to a cut and a splice, usually under an hour after the wall is open.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Mountain View

    Pinhole leaks in copper rarely show up alone. Water chemistry, high flow velocity and contact with soil under a slab can all pit the pipe wall from the inside, and once a few pinholes appear, more are usually on the way. Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running hard. A switch to PEX ends it. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Mountain View

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Mountain View

    The main from the meter to the house is the one line everything else depends on.
