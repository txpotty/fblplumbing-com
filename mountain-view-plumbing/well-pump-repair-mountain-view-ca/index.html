    Pressure Tank Repair in Mountain View, CA

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. Fast cycling kills pumps young. We check the air charge, inspect the bladder and recharge or replace the tank, then confirm the pump runs at least a minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Mountain View, CA

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. When they fault, the drive shows a code that guides diagnosis. We troubleshoot the drive, the pressure sensor and the wiring, then repair or replace the failed part and verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Mountain View, CA

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Northern California code when the job is done.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Mountain View

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. When the bladder fails, the tank fills with water and the pump starts and stops every time someone opens a faucet. The bladder, the switch and the tank fittings all get checked. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Mountain View

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. Testing starts at the surface. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Mountain View

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.
