    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Mountain View

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet usually costs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. Nights and weekends run higher.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. It works well on hair, wipes and roots. Jetting scours the whole pipe with high-pressure water and strips the grease, scale and sludge a cable slides past. For recurring clogs, jetting is the better bet.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting cleans more. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the typical signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. That one is serious. Frequent clogs in the same line also point to a deeper blockage, often roots at a joint 30 or 40 feet out from the house.

    Routine drain cleaning in Mountain View, CA does not usually need a permit. Major repair, sewer replacement or excavation in the yard generally does. We handle that paperwork. You will know about any permit before repair work is scheduled.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. A root-packed main or a job that adds jetting can take most of a day. Access makes the biggest difference. A buried or missing cleanout means pulling a toilet first, which adds about an hour.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Collapsed or badly cracked pipe can be hurt by anything run through it. The tech's touch matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.
