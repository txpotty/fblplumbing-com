    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Mountain View

    Most visits in Mountain View run somewhere between $200 and $800, set mostly by trap size and how long it has gone since the last pump-out. Access matters too. A trap behind a walk-in that needs 80 feet of hose costs more than one sitting next to the curb. You get the price first.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. You do need to keep the records. Installing a new trap or interceptor, or changing the size of one, usually needs a plumbing permit and sometimes a pretreatment program review before the work starts.

    Slow sinks come first. Then gurgling floor drains and a sour smell near the dish area. Gray water rising through a floor sink means the trap is far past 25 percent and grease is already moving downstream.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Plan one to three hours for an outdoor interceptor. Big tanks with a hardened cap take the longest, since the cap has to be broken up with a pressure wand before the hose will pull it.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. The industry calls it brown grease. It is different from the yellow grease in your used fryer oil bin, which is recycled separately and should never be poured into the trap.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Plenty of busy restaurants pump monthly. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. Warm, it pours like water, and cool, it sets like wax. In a sewer pipe it sticks to the walls in layers, catches food scraps and wipes, and eventually closes the pipe down to a pencil-sized opening.
