    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Antelope

    A simple solder repair usually runs $200 to $500. Access adds cost. A slab leak that needs specialized detection and repair could range from $800 to $3,000 or more, with reroutes at the top of that range. We price it once we know the cause.

    We work with Type K, L and M copper from all standard manufacturers. All are code-listed. We repair whatever is already in your walls, and we tie new copper or PEX into it with fittings rated for the pipe size and the transition. We default to Type L.

    Wall thickness. Type K is the heaviest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, and Type M has the thinnest wall, which is why it tends to pinhole first in older homes. Our repairs use Type L.

    We typically use Type L copper. It is heavier than Type M. Type L lasts longer than the Type M found in many older homes, and in some spots we use ProPress fittings or a PEX transition instead of solder. All materials are potable-rated.

    An accessible pinhole or a single joint takes one to four hours. Hidden leaks add time. Leaks under a slab or a repipe can take a full day or more, depending on access, whether the line is rerouted and how much copper needs replacing. The quote includes a time estimate.

    A small isolated repair usually does not need one. Repipes often do. Replacing a large section, rerouting lines or working on the main water supply in Antelope may require a plumbing permit, which allows an inspection before anything is covered. The permit is on us.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. They will not last. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, since a failed patch at full pressure can soak a floor in minutes. Use it only until a plumber arrives.

    The pipe wall wears through from the inside. Water quality is the big factor. Aggressive water, high velocity, excess pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues each thin the wall until it pins through. Hot water speeds it up.
