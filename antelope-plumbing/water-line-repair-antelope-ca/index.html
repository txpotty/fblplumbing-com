    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Antelope

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Newer homes across Northern California mostly use PEX or copper with plastic drains, and many owners are repiping older houses to improve water pressure. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Antelope

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Main Water Line Repair in Antelope

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Antelope

    Underground leaks hide well. Many people find out from the bill before they see anything in the yard. Others notice a patch of grass that grows greener than the rest, or ground that stays soft long after the sprinklers shut off. We confirm the leak at the meter, trace the pipe route and listen along it. We dig where the noise is loudest.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Antelope

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    PEX Water Line Repair in Antelope, CA

    PEX itself rarely fails underground, but the joints and the first few feet near the house take a beating. Rats and gophers sometimes chew it. A shovel or a fence post auger can nick it, and sunlight exposure before burial weakens the plastic over time. We cut out the damaged section and install new pipe with crimp, clamp or expansion fittings, matched to the PEX brand already in the ground.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Antelope

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. Depth drives most of that. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, while trenchless replacement tends to run $3,000 to $7,000 since it needs a boring rig or puller. The written price comes before we start.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, which most residential service lines are.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    Small spot repairs often do not need one. Full replacements usually do. Rules vary by jurisdiction in Antelope, so FBL Plumbing checks the local requirement for your address and pulls the permit when one applies. The inspector sees the trench before it is backfilled.

    Labor is warrantied, always. New pipe and fittings carry the manufacturer warranty as well, and both terms are written on your estimate before any work starts. Keep the invoice. You will want it if anything comes up.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner owns the line to the house. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so we confirm where the leak sits before quoting anything. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.

    Fix small leaks early. Little leaks grow. Check your pressure once a year, because a bad regulator stresses every pipe in the house, and keep big-rooted trees off the pipe route. For older homes in Antelope, replacing an aging galvanized line on your own schedule usually costs less than an emergency.
