    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Antelope

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and confirm the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Antelope, CA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to suit your new tank's pre-charge.

    A new well pressure tank mainly helps maintain consistent water pressure and reduces pump cycling.     Common signs include your well pump constantly turning on and off (short cycling), fluctuating water pressure, air sputtering from faucets, or a tank that is waterlogged (feels heavy when tapped and full of water). If you notice any of these, it is time for an inspection.

    On average, a well pressure tank can last between 5 to 10 years, though some high-quality tanks can last longer. Factors like water quality, proper initial installation, and regular maintenance can significantly impact its lifespan.

    Most pressure tanks last 10 to 15 years. Hard water, sand and frequent cycling can shorten that, especially when the pre-charge is never checked.
