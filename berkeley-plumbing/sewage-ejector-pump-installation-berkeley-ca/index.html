    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Berkeley

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Zoeller Ejector Pump Installation in Berkeley, CA

    Zoeller has made ejector pumps for decades, and its cast iron models are the ones we see still running in older basements. They work well for new systems and for drop-in replacements, since many Zoeller models fit existing pits and discharge piping as is. Floats and switches are stocked locally.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Berkeley

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. The slab gets repoured.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Berkeley, CA

    Businesses in Berkeley, CA with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Closed restrooms cost business. Each pump can carry the load alone.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Berkeley

    Residential ejector systems in Berkeley serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Berkeley

    Macerating systems like Saniflo let you add a toilet and sink without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet, grinds the waste and pumps it through a small discharge line, often three-quarter inch or one inch, up to the nearest drain, which makes it a good fit for a small basement half bath. Venting still applies.
