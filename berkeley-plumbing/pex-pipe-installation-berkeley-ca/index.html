    Leak Repair with PEX in Berkeley, CA

    For individual pipe leaks, PEX can be used to repair or replace sections of damaged piping. Our plumbers can isolate the compromised section and tie in PEX, providing a durable and flexible fix that holds up to future pressures. This targeted approach is cost-effective for localized issues and brings the benefits of PEX to troubled areas of your existing plumbing in Berkeley, CA.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Berkeley

    PEX-B is stiffer and more affordable, and it is joined with copper crimp rings or stainless steel cinch clamps over brass or poly fittings. It is a good option for smaller projects and budget-conscious repipes. Brands like Viega, Zurn and SharkBite offer dependable PEX-B systems. We use a go/no-go gauge on every crimp to confirm a proper seal.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Berkeley, CA

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Plenty of insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to sell and insure.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Berkeley

    If your home has old, corroded or leaking pipes, a full home PEX repipe can greatly improve your plumbing system. We remove outdated galvanized, copper or polybutylene pipes and replace them with durable PEX, upgrading your entire water delivery system. This prevents future leaks, improves water pressure and ends the rust-colored water often seen with older piping in Berkeley.

    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Berkeley, CA

    A manifold system sends a dedicated line from a central hub to each fixture, much like a breaker panel for water. Each line gets its own shutoff, which makes repairs easier and keeps pressure steady when multiple fixtures run. Home-run layouts use more pipe but fewer fittings in walls. We often combine manifold and trunk-and-branch designs to weigh cost and performance.
