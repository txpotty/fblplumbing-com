    Dependable Water Softener Replacement Services in Berkeley, CA

    Metered Softener Replacement in Berkeley

    Older timer-based softeners regenerate on a set schedule whether you used water or not. Metered softeners track actual usage and regenerate only when the resin is nearly used up. This saves salt, water and wear on the system. We replace old timer valves with metered Fleck 5600SXT or Clack WS1 valves. Most homeowners notice lower salt use within the first month.

    Clack Water Softener Replacement in Berkeley

    Clack valves are a popular choice for their reliability and customizable settings. If you have an older Clack system failing, FBL Plumbing offers professional Clack water softener replacement in Berkeley. We can replace just the control valve or the entire system, ensuring compatibility with your existing setup and configuring the new unit for peak performance.

    Dual Tank Softener Systems in Berkeley

    Large households and homes with very hard water can benefit from a dual tank system that provides soft water all day and night. While one tank regenerates, the other stays in service. Kinetico and similar non-electric twin tank systems are popular for this reason. We size dual tank systems to your peak demand. They cost more upfront but prevent hard water bleed-through during regeneration.

    Replacing Fleck Water Softeners in Berkeley, CA

    Fleck is a well-known brand for dependable water softener control valves, found in many residential and commercial systems across Northern California. If your Fleck-controlled softener is beyond repair or simply outdated, FBL Plumbing can properly replace the entire unit. We are familiar with many Fleck models and can recommend an equivalent or upgraded system that fits seamlessly with your existing plumbing in Berkeley.

    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Berkeley, CA

    Softener resin can break down after 10 to 15 years, especially with chlorinated city water. When resin fails, you will see hard water symptoms even though the system is regenerating. Sometimes replacing resin and the tank is more economical than a whole new system. We inspect the valve and brine tank to decide. If the valve is old, a full replacement often makes more sense.
