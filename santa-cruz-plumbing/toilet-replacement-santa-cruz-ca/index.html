    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Santa Cruz

    The cost of toilet replacement in Santa Cruz, CA usually ranges from $400 to $800, including the new toilet fixture and labor. This can vary based on the toilet model you choose, any necessary repairs to the flange or subfloor, and the complexity of the installation.

    We recommend dependable brands known for their quality, efficiency, and availability of parts. Popular choices include Kohler, American Standard, Toto, Delta, and Gerber. Each offers a range of styles and features, from basic models to advanced low-flow or comfort height options. We can help you select the right fit for your needs.

    A like-for-like toilet replacement in the same location generally does not require a permit. If drains are moved or a new bathroom is added, a permit is usually required.

    Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup in toilet tanks and bowls, affecting flush mechanisms and causing stains. When replacing a toilet, we can recommend models with surfaces designed to fight mineral buildup and discuss water softening options.

    FBL Plumbing provides a warranty on both our workmanship and the installed toilet fixture. The specific terms vary by toilet brand, but we stand behind our installations to ensure your peace of mind regarding leaks or malfunctions.

    Most standard toilet replacements can be completed by a professional plumber in about 2 to 4 hours. This includes the removal of the old unit, preparation of the flange, installation of the new toilet, and testing for leaks. More complex installations might take a bit longer.

    Key signs include persistent leaks that are difficult to pinpoint or fix, a cracked toilet bowl or tank, frequent clogs despite proper use, excessive rocking or instability, or a toilet that is very old and inefficient, using too much water per flush. If repair costs approach the price of a new toilet, replacement is often the better choice.

    The rough-in measurement refers to the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drainpipe (the bolts that secure the toilet). Standard rough-ins are typically 12 inches, but 10-inch and 14-inch options exist. Knowing your rough-in is crucial to make sure your new toilet fits correctly in the existing space.
