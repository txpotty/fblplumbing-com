    Frequently Asked Questions About Storm Drain Cleaning in Santa Cruz

    Residential storm drain cleaning in Santa Cruz usually runs $200 to $500 depending on the number of inlets and pipe length. Dry well cleaning and commercial systems typically cost more and are quoted after a site visit.

    We recommend having your storm drains inspected and possibly cleaned once a year, especially if you have mature trees nearby. Tree roots seek out sewer laterals during dry spells, which is why heavy rains often reveal cracked lines through sudden backups. Properties prone to debris buildup or with a history of clogs may benefit from twice-yearly cleaning to prevent major issues.

    Hydro jetting is a powerful drain cleaning method that uses high-pressure water streams to blast away stubborn blockages and thoroughly clean the interior of drain pipes. For storm drains, it is highly effective at removing compacted soil, heavy silt, grease, and roots, restoring the drain to its full flow capacity much better than snaking.

    While you can remove surface debris from drain grates, deeply clogged storm drains require professional tools like hydro jetters and camera equipment for effective cleaning. DIY attempts can sometimes push debris further down or damage the pipes, leading to more costly repairs. It is safer and more reliable to hire a professional.

    To prevent storm drain clogs, regularly clear leaves and debris from grates and catch basins, avoid planting trees directly over drain lines, and keep landscaping in good shape to prevent soil erosion. Consider installing grates with smaller openings in high-debris areas and schedule yearly professional cleanings.

    In Santa Cruz, common causes of storm drain clogs include built-up leaves, dirt, and organic debris from trees, silt and sediment from soil erosion, trash, and sadly, sometimes even illegally dumped waste. Tree root intrusion into the pipes is also a significant factor, especially in older systems.

    We use water jetters, vacuum equipment and push cameras built for drain work. These tools remove debris without damaging thin-wall storm pipe.

    Common signs include water pooling around your property after rain, slow drainage from downspouts, visible debris collecting at catch basin grates, and unusual odors coming from the drains. If you notice any of these, it is a good idea to have your storm drains inspected.
