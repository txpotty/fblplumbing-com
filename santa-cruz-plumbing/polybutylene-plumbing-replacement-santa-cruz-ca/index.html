    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Santa Cruz

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. Watch the water meter. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random. Once we find it, a temporary fix buys time to schedule the repipe.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Santa Cruz

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Santa Cruz

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX is flexible, handles freezing and movement better and installs faster, while copper is rigid, tolerates heat and sunlight and has a long track record, so the right answer depends on budget, water and preference.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It takes skill and time. Hidden leaks, failed inspections and voided warranties are common results of DIY repipes, so this one is best left to licensed plumbers.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. Manufacturer coverage adds more. You get both warranties in writing at the end of the job, along with photos and the permit record, so everything is in one folder if you ever need it.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. Homes built from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s are most likely to have it.

    Most of our PEX replacements use Uponor AquaPEX with ProPEX expansion fittings. Viega PEX is another option. For copper, we use domestic Type L pipe, and we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    Replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. Licensed crews work faster and leave you warranties and paperwork.
