    Exterior Mount Tankless Units in Clovis, CA

    Exterior-mount tankless units hang on an outside wall and vent straight out the front, so there is no vent pipe to run. Sun and weather wear on the covers and wiring. Mountain and foothill homes in Northern California see real winters, so pipes there need insulation, heat tape, or careful draining when a cabin sits empty.     Gas Line Testing on Tankless Installs in Clovis, CA

    Every gas connection on a tankless gets a soap test and a manometer reading at full fire before we leave the house. Static pressure means little if it drops when the burner opens up. Both readings get taken.

    Rinnai Condensing Upgrades in Clovis, CA

    Rinnai Sensei condensing models replace older non-condensing Rinnai units in a lot of homes, but the venting changes from stainless to PVC. The vent swap is part of our job. Gas and water connections usually line up.

    Commercial Tankless Racks in Clovis, CA

    Restaurants and salons run tankless racks hard. Four units together can pull over 600,000 BTU, which means checking gas pressure with all of them running. Installs can happen after closing.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Clovis, CA

    Pulling a 50 gallon tank out of a garage frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss. The catch is gas, since most tankless units need a 3/4-inch line or bigger plus new venting. We check the meter size and pipe run before quoting, and we cap or reroute the old flue and seal the roof penetration if it is no longer needed. Plan on a full day.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Clovis

    Cascade controls on a commercial rack decide which unit fires first, and after one unit is replaced they need rebalancing. Otherwise one unit does all the work. We also set a descaling schedule.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Clovis

    Condensing Tankless Replacement in Clovis

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE or Rinnai Sensei pass 90 percent efficiency by pulling extra heat out of the exhaust. They vent through PVC. The condensate is acidic, so we install a neutralizer cartridge and run the line to an approved drain. Inland valleys get hot summers that work water heaters and exterior lines hard, while coastal areas stay mild and damp year round. The savings carry through every season because hot water use does.
