    Urgent Water Softener Repair in Castro Valley, CA

    Injector and Brine Line Cleaning in Castro Valley, CA

    The injector, sometimes called a venturi, creates the suction that draws brine into the resin tank. Sediment and salt residue clog its tiny ports. Once it clogs, the brine tank fills with water instead of emptying, and the softener regenerates with plain water that does nothing to the resin. The injector and screen get cleaned or replaced, and the brine line gets checked.

    Brine Tank and Salt Bridge Repair in Castro Valley, CA

    A crust of salt across the brine tank stops regeneration, and hard water shows up at every tap within a day or two. The bridge gets broken up and the tank cleaned out. Cracked brine tanks on older units get replaced. Keep the salt about half full.

    Salt Bridge and Brine Tank Repair in Castro Valley

    Salt bridges form when a hard crust builds across the brine tank and the salt below it stops dissolving into the water. Salt sits there unused, and regeneration fails. We break the bridge, clean out the mushy salt and check the float and brine well. A cracked brine tank gets swapped for a new one.

    No-Salt Water Conditioner Repair in Castro Valley, CA

    No-salt conditioners do not remove minerals, they change how scale forms, but they still need service. If scale comes back or pressure drops, the media or the prefilter is usually the problem. FBL Plumbing repairs these units in Castro Valley, CA. The media wears out in a few years.

    Softener Repair vs Replacement Cost in Castro Valley, CA

    Most softener repairs cost $150 to $450, while a resin replacement often runs $400 to $900 depending on tank size. Installing a new quality softener usually runs $1,500 to $3,500. With a good tank and valve, repair is the better value. If the valve is obsolete and parts are scarce, replacing the head or the whole unit may make more sense than chasing used parts online.

    Salt Usage and Settings Repair in Castro Valley

    Too much salt or water use usually means settings that do not match your hardness or household. Older timer units regenerate on a schedule no matter how much water you used. After retesting hardness, we reprogram the valve or swap in a metered head that regenerates only when needed. Salt bills drop.
