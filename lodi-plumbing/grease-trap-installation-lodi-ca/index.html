    Under-Sink Grease Trap Setup in Lodi, CA

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Lodi, CA

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where.     Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Lodi

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more once excavation and site restoration are counted. Brand is a small part of it. The written estimate holds unless the dig turns up a buried surprise.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. Our time on site is usually one day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. We like the plastic bodies from Schier where steel would rust out. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. For fry-heavy kitchens we also set automatic units that skim grease on a timer. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    Nearly every food business needs one. Plumbing codes require a grease interceptor wherever fixtures discharge grease-laden waste, which covers restaurants, bakeries and most food prep spaces. Some coffee shops and prepackaged food counters get a waiver, but that decision belongs to the local pretreatment office, not the landlord or the previous tenant. Ask before you sign the lease.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. The work needs a licensed plumber and a permit in nearly every jurisdiction. For a home kitchen you might be allowed to, as long as the flow control and venting are done correctly. Ask the building department first.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.
