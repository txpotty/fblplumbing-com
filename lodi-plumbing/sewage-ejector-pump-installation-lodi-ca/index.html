    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lodi

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin typically costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work typically ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can affect its lifespan. Annual inspections can help extend its longevity.

    A sewage ejector pump is designed to handle solid waste and wastewater from plumbing fixtures below the main sewer line, like toilets and sinks, discharging it into the sewer system. A sump pump, in contrast, removes groundwater from a basement or crawl space, only handling clear water to prevent flooding. They serve distinct purposes for different types of water.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump typically has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help extend its operating life.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation typically takes one to two days. This includes excavating the pit, setting the basin and pump, connecting the plumbing and completing the electrical hookups. More complex installations, like those requiring extensive trenching or electrical panel upgrades, may take longer.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that needs immediate attention.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can identify potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is particularly important for older units.
