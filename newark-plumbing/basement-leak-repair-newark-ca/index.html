    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Newark

    Basement plumbing leak repairs in Newark typically cost $200 to $1,500, depending on the pipe and how hard it is to reach. Exposed pipe on an unfinished ceiling is cheap. Pipe behind finished drywall or under the slab adds the cost of opening and patching, and a cast iron stack replacement can run higher than the range above if it goes through more than one floor.

    For sump pumps we mostly install Zoeller and Liberty, with Wayne for some battery backups. We prefer cast iron pump housings. Injection resins and drain membranes come from commercial suppliers, matched so the parts fit.

    Only some repairs need excavation. Outside membranes and new footing drains require digging to the footing. Pipe repairs, crack injection and interior drain systems are all done from inside the basement, which is why they are the more common choice on homes with patios, driveways or landscaping right against the foundation.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Test the sump each spring and swap rubber washer hoses for braided ones every five years.

    Start outside. Clean gutters and push downspout discharge six feet or more from the wall. Make sure the soil slopes away from the house, test the sump pump each spring by filling the pit with a bucket of water, and insulate cold water lines that sweat in humid weather. Check the water heater yearly.

    Simple pipe repairs and crack injection usually do not need a permit. Larger work is a different story. Stack replacement, new sumps and backwater valves often need one, and we handle it.

    A pipe or fitting repair usually takes an hour or two. Crack injection takes about half a day. Sump pit installation takes most of a day, and an interior perimeter drain system takes two to five days depending on wall length and how much finished space has to be cleared first.

    Most supply and drain repairs are finished in a few hours. A new sump takes most of a day. Drain stack replacements and slab leak repairs usually take one to two days, since they involve opening finished surfaces, letting any new concrete patch cure, and running a final test before we close things up.
