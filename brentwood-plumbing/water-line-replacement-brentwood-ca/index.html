    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Replacement in Brentwood

    Price follows the run. Short runs in easy ground cost the least. Most homes land somewhere between $3,000 and $15,000, and the spread comes from pipe material, line length, depth, rock or concrete in the path, and whether trenchless is possible. We price it after seeing both ends of the line.

    Repair fixes one spot. We cut out the bad section and splice in new pipe. Replacement swaps the entire run from meter to house, which is the better choice once the old pipe is corroded everywhere or has failed more than once, because another weak spot is likely close by.

    Pressure that drops at every fixture at once is the big one. So is a patch of grass that stays soggy when nothing else is wet. Add rusty water after a shutoff, a water bill that climbs without new usage, or a meter dial that spins with every tap closed, and we would test the main first. We test that first.

    Copper laid in decent soil commonly lasts 50 years and often longer. PEX carries long manufacturer warranties too. The things that shorten either one, in our experience digging up failed lines, are aggressive soil chemistry, sharp rock against the pipe, and poor bedding. Good install, long life.

    Inland valleys get hot summers that work water heaters and exterior lines hard, while coastal areas stay mild and damp year round. Freezing is uncommon along the coast and on valley floors in Northern California, but frosty nights do happen and can crack unprotected outdoor pipes. Depth is the main defense against freezing. We set new service lines at or below the frost depth local code requires, insulate the riser where it comes up to the meter, and use pipe that tolerates the swings, which in cold country often means PEX.

    Both are good. PEX is cheaper and handles long runs with no buried fittings. Copper is stiffer, tolerates high pressure and has a long track record, but it can pit in some soil and water chemistries, so we look at what has failed in your neighborhood before recommending one. Price matters too.
