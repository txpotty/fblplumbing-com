    PEX can be used for outdoor applications like irrigation lines or outdoor spigots, but it needs to be protected from direct UV light, which can break down the material over time. When used outdoors, PEX should consistently be buried or otherwise shielded.

    Most whole-home PEX repipes take two to four days. Larger homes or difficult attic access can take a bit longer.

    PEX is a good fit for a wide range of climates because of its flexibility and resistance to freezing. Wet winters and long dry summers define the plumbing calendar in Northern California, with drainage problems in the rainy season and outdoor leaks in summer. While rigid pipes can burst when water freezes, PEX can expand and contract, greatly lowering the risk of burst pipes during cold snaps. It also performs well in high temperatures, making it a reliable choice year-round in Brentwood.

    PEX must be protected from sunlight because UV light breaks down it. Outdoors it needs to be buried, sleeved or completely covered.

    While PEX is simpler to install than copper, professional installation is highly recommended. Correct sizing, routing and secure connections are essential for preventing leaks and ensuring system longevity. Improper DIY installation can lead to costly water damage and code violations.

    PEX offers several advantages over copper, including lower material and installation costs, greater flexibility and resistance to corrosion and freezing.
