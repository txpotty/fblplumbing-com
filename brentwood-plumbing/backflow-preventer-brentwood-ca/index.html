    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Brentwood

    Testing in Brentwood, CA usually costs between $75 and $150 per assembly. The price includes the trip, a calibrated test and sending the report to your provider. Multiple assemblies at one property cost less each, since the tester is already on site with the gauge hooked up. Commercial lines cost more.

    Ten to twenty years is common for a well-kept brass assembly. How long it lasts depends on the water, the weather it sits in and the kind of device. Freezing is uncommon along the coast and on valley floors in Northern California, but frosty nights do happen and can crack unprotected outdoor pipes. Yearly testing extends its life.

    Yes, most new installs and replacements need a plumbing permit from the Brentwood building department. The inspector checks the device type, height and clearance, and the water provider usually wants an initial test report on file too. We pull the permit and file the test.

    Once a year is the usual requirement. Some providers require it after installation, repair or relocation as well. Your provider's notice will list the deadline, and we can usually test within a few days of seeing it, sometimes sooner.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco are the most common, and we also see Apollo and Zurn on commercial work. Common kits ride along.

    A brass assembly often lasts 10 to 20 years, sometimes longer. The rubber parts inside wear out much sooner, which is why rebuild kits exist and why most failed tests are fixed without replacing the body. Cold snaps are hard on them.

    Backflow preventers stop contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply during pressure drops. Sprinkler systems, fire lines and commercial equipment are the usual sources, and providers require protection on all of them. It is the law in most places.

    Main breaks, hydrant use and fire flow can drop city water pressure in seconds. When that happens, any cross-connection, a sprinkler line, a boiler or a hose in a bucket, can pull water back toward the main. The assembly is the barrier.
