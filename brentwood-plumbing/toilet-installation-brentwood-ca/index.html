    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Brentwood

    Labor to install a customer-supplied standard toilet usually runs $200 to $400. Including a mid-range toilet, most installs total $450 to $900, while wall-hung systems with carriers range from $1,500 to $3,500 or more. We give you a flat price up front.

    Homes on hard water see scale on fixtures and water heaters, while very soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper pipe. Hard water can lead to mineral buildup in toilet tanks and bowls over time, affecting performance and requiring more regular cleaning. While it doesn't directly impact installation, choosing a toilet with a good glaze and strong flush can help reduce these effects.

    FBL Plumbing installs all major toilet brands, including Kohler, Toto, American Standard, Delta, and Gerber. We can help you choose a model that fits your budget, style preferences, and water efficiency goals.

    While it's possible for a homeowner to attempt a DIY toilet installation, it's usually best left to professionals. Improper installation can lead to leaks, wobbling, inefficient flushing, and potential water damage. Professional plumbers deliver proper sealing, secure mounting, and adherence to codes.

    Toilets carry manufacturer warranties, often 1 to 5 years on parts and longer on porcelain. We also guarantee our installation workmanship and will return to fix any leak caused by the install.

    A one-piece toilet has the bowl and tank integrated into a single unit, offering a sleek look and simpler cleaning. A two-piece toilet has a separate bowl and tank, which are bolted together during installation. Both types are easy to find and perform well.

    Replacing a toilet in the same location generally does not need a permit, but new bathrooms, additions and drain relocations typically do. Requirements vary by city, and we check with the local building department for your project.

    Common signs that you might need a new toilet include frequent clogs, constant running, visible cracks in the bowl or tank, excessive wobbling, or a toilet that is very old and inefficient. If you're always repairing it, replacement is often more cost-effective.
