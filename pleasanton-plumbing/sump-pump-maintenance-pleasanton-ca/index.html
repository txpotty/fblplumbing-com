    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Pleasanton, CA

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Next, we cut power to the main pump and watch the backup clear the basin by itself. Alarms get tested too.

    Home Sump Pump Maintenance in Pleasanton

    A typical house pit is about 18 inches across and two feet deep, and most of what goes wrong happens inside it. We pull the pump, clear the intake screen and scoop out the silt.     Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Pleasanton

    Tree roots seek out sewer laterals during dry spells, which is why heavy rains often reveal cracked lines through sudden backups. We test the pump a few weeks before the wet season by filling the pit with a hose until the float trips. Then we time how fast it pumps out and listen to the bearings. A struggling pump can then be replaced on a calm day instead of in the middle of a storm.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Pleasanton, CA

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. We check the rod for bends and the stops for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Pleasanton, CA

    Some Wayne models draw water through a top suction intake, which helps them avoid pulling in the sludge that settles at the bottom of a pit. That intake gets cleaned, and so does the float mechanism. Any brand can rattle its discharge fittings loose over time. Easy fix.

    Battery Backup System Testing in Pleasanton, CA

    Storms knock out power when the pit fills fastest. A backup pump exists for exactly that hour. Our plumber reads the battery date, cleans corrosion off the terminals and tests the charger, because a failed charger can leave the battery flat within a season.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without it, the pump repeats its work. All that extra cycling shortens motor life and adds to the power bill.
