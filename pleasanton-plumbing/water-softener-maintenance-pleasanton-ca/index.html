    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Pleasanton

    Resin does the actual softening. Each bead trades sodium for calcium and magnesium until it runs out of capacity. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Pleasanton

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. Between those, the salt level, brine well and float all get a look. It takes about an hour on most homes.

    Salt Bridge Removal and Brine Tank Cleaning in Pleasanton, CA

    Humidity and cheap salt cause most salt bridges. The top layer fuses into a crust with an empty pocket below it. The softener keeps regenerating on schedule, but it draws plain water instead of brine, so the resin never recharges and hard water flows to every tap.

    Resin Rebedding in Pleasanton

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we empty out the old resin and refill the tank with new high-capacity media, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Bypass Valve and Drain Line Repairs in Pleasanton, CA

    A bypass valve that does not seat fully lets raw water blend into the softened supply, and the hardness test is usually the first clue. We rebuild or replace the bypass and check the drain line for kinks. The drain line has to end above its receptor with an air gap.

    Commercial Twin-Tank Maintenance in Pleasanton

    Salons, car washes, hotels and restaurants put far more gallons through a softener than any house does, and a hard-water day can mean spotted glassware or a scaled ice machine. Twin-tank and progressive-flow systems need closer checks on the meter and the tank changeover. Visits run monthly or quarterly, depending on volume.

    Full-System Softener Inspections in Pleasanton

    An inspection covers more than the two tanks. The inlet and outlet piping, bypass, drain line and brine overflow all get traced and checked. Green crust on copper fittings, a drip at the bypass or a drain line jammed down into a standpipe all go on the written report.
