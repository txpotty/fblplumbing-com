    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Alameda

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. With hydro jetting added, expect roughly $450 to $1,000, depending on how long the line is and how heavy the grease is. Nights and weekends run higher.

    We run RIDGID, Spartan and Gorlitz augers, plus sewer cameras and jetters from the same tier of commercial makers. They survive heavy daily use. Brand matters less than setup. Even a top machine can hurt a pipe with the wrong head, so picking the cutter gets as much attention as the machine.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. They hold up well to heavy daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    It can be, so we prepare for it. We lay cloths from the entry to the drain and keep buckets and rags at the cleanout. Cables come out wet. It gets wiped as it comes out, and debris goes into bags before we clean up and leave.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Alameda's older homes suffer the worst of it. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. It works well on hair, wipes and roots. Hydro jetting washes the entire inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, removing grease, scale and sludge the cable leaves on the walls. For recurring clogs, jetting is the better bet.

    Most rooter jobs include a limited guarantee for a set number of days. If the line clogs again within that time, we return at no extra charge.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.
