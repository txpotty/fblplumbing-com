    Sewer Line Replacement Methods in Alameda, CA

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Alameda, CA

    Most of our sewer work is single-family homes. Expansive clay soils are common across Northern California, and seasonal swelling and shrinking can crack slabs and buried sewer laterals. The usual suspects are rooted clay joints, cast iron worn through at the bottom and sections that sank as the soil settled. Replacing the line stops sewage from backing up into the lowest drain in the house.

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Alameda, CA

    Concrete is expensive to replace. With pipe bursting, the new line goes in under the driveway or tree from one pit to another, following the old pipe's route. We fuse the HDPE into one continuous length on the lawn. Most bursts finish in a day. You end up patching two small pits rather than a whole slab.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement in Alameda, CA

    Two holes instead of one long trench. Pipe bursting drags a cone-shaped head through the old line, cracking it outward while new fused HDPE follows right behind. Lining is the other trenchless route. A resin-soaked tube is pulled into the cleaned host pipe and cured in place, which leaves a new pipe inside the old one and works well when the original still holds its round shape.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Alameda

    No replacement quote without video. We run a self-leveling color camera from the cleanout to the main and record every joint, crack, root mass and low spot along the way, with footage you keep. A sonde in the camera head lets us mark the depth on the lawn. The footage tells us whether one section or the whole line needs to go.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Alameda, CA

    Sometimes one joint is the whole problem. With only one bad section, we excavate a single small pit and splice in new PVC using shielded couplings. Far cheaper than a full run. We only suggest it when the footage backs it up.

    Commercial Sewer Lateral Work in Alameda, CA

Outside the foundation we replace it with PVC or HDPE. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.
