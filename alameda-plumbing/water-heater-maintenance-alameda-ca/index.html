    Flushing a Tank Water Heater in Alameda

    Popping and rumbling mean sediment. Calcium and magnesium settle on the bottom of the tank and form a layer that traps heat under it, so water boils in pockets beneath the scale and pops every time the burner or lower element fires. The tank drains until the water is clean. Bad tanks need a few rounds.

    Tank Water Heater Maintenance in Alameda

    Flushing is the heart of tank maintenance. Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running mineral-heavy. We run a hose from the drain valve, open it up and let the tank drain until the water runs clear, then refill and check the anode rod, the relief valve and every connection for drips or corrosion. Takes about an hour. A clean tank heats faster and lasts longer.

    Attic, Closet and Garage Units in Alameda

    Location changes the risks. Attic heaters need pans and drain lines, and garage gas units often need elevation or protection. Pans get inspected. Access platforms and venting get looked at too.

    Yearly Service Plans in Alameda, CA

    Anode Rod Checks in Alameda

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. It is a long metal rod screwed into the top of the tank, and once it has corroded down to bare wire, the water starts eating the steel tank lining instead. It comes out for inspection. Replacing the rod is much cheaper than replacing the heater.

    Electric Element Service in Alameda, CA

    Two elements, two thermostats. A burned-out lower element is the usual cause of running short on hot water. We test with power off. Good parts stay in.

    Water Heater Leak Checks in Alameda, CA

    Small drips become floods. Connections, the relief valve, the drain valve and the seams get checked, along with the pan and floor. Rust stains are a warning. Catching it early saves the floor.

    Relief Valve and Safety Testing in Alameda, CA

    The relief valve is the tank's last line of defense. We test the valve, confirm it reseats and check that the discharge pipe is routed correctly. Leaky valves get replaced. Expansion tanks get an air charge check.
