    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Alameda

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Hidden leaks cost more. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, depending on where the leak is, how bad it is and how much copper needs replacing. We price it after we find it.

    We typically use Type L copper. It is heavier than Type M. Type L lasts longer than the Type M found in many older homes, while press fittings or a PEX transition get used when a torch is a bad idea. Every part is lead-free.

    Every repair is warrantied. We write the warranty down. Our workmanship warranty covers the repair, and copper pipe and fittings carry their own manufacturer warranties on top of it. You see the terms first.

    A visible pinhole or a small section is usually done in two to four hours. Slab work takes longer. Slab leaks or a repipe can take a full day or more, with the method and access setting the pace. We tell you up front.

    We work with Type K, L and M copper from all standard manufacturers. All are code-listed. We repair whatever is already in your walls, and we tie new copper or PEX into it with fittings rated for the pipe size and the transition. Type L is our usual repair pipe.

    Wall thickness. K is thickest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, and Type M has the thinnest wall, which is why it tends to pinhole first in older homes. Our repairs use Type L.

    Wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors are the obvious sign. Pressure drops count too. The sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps, and green deposits on pipe you can see all suggest a leak. Check the meter with everything off.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Not the pipe itself. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, while damaged drywall, flooring and cabinets may be covered depending on your policy. Ask your insurer. We provide documentation.
