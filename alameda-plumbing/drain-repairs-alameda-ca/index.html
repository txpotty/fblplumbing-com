    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Alameda

    A simple clog clearing starts around $150. Main line work costs more. Complex main line repairs can run past $3,000, depending on the drain involved, the extent of the damage and the access. We price it after we look.

    We install PVC and ABS for most new drain work. Cast iron where needed. Cast iron still makes sense in some multi-story walls where noise matters, and we tie new pipe into existing metal drains with listed transition fittings. Every part meets code.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Grease or scale is common. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge will snag paper and hair until the line closes off again a few weeks after each cleaning. We find it on video.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, so a drain that clogs repeatedly usually needs repair rather than more snaking. A camera tells you which.

    Age and corrosion lead the list. Soil movement is close behind. Tree roots, temperature swings, heavy loads over buried pipe, plus bad installation such as skipped primer or unsupported runs, can all break a drain early. It is usually a combination.

    Slow drains, gurgling and sewer smell are the big three warnings. Drips in the cabinet count too. A drain that clogs again a few weeks after it was cleared, or a stain on the ceiling below a tub or shower, means the pipe or a fitting needs repair rather than another cleaning. Catch it early.

    Tightening a slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is fair game. Hand snakes are fine too. Anything inside a wall, under a floor or involving cast iron, or a leak that keeps returning after you have tightened it, is a job for a licensed plumber with the right tools. Skip the caustic cleaners.

    Most trap and fixture drain repairs take one to two hours. Wall work takes longer. Replacing drain pipe inside a wall or under a floor can take half a day to a full day, depending on how much finish material has to come out. The estimate includes the timeline.
