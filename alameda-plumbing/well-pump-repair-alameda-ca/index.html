    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Alameda, CA

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Northern California code before we restart the pump.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Alameda, CA

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. Our technicians test circuits with the power locked out. All wiring and splices meet Northern California electrical code before the pump restarts.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Alameda, CA

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. FBL Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Alameda

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Well Pump Repair vs Replacement Cost in Alameda, CA

    Switch and control repairs often cost $150 to $500, while a pressure tank replacement typically runs $600 to $1,500. A submersible pull and replacement runs about $1,800 to $5,000 and up, based on depth and horsepower. Replacing an aging pump during a pull saves paying for a second pull later. You see both numbers.

    Low Water Pressure Diagnosis in Alameda

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Alameda

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. Precharge gets checked on an empty tank. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Alameda

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. Testing starts at the surface. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.
