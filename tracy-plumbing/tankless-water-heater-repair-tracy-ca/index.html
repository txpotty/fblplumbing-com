    Tankless Descaling and Flushing in Tracy

    Rinnai shows an LC code and Navien posts a scale warning when the exchanger runs hot from mineral buildup. Clear the code without flushing and it comes right back, because the scale is still in there. We connect a circulation pump to the service valves, run descaler through the exchanger for about an hour, then flush and clean the inlet screen. Yearly is right for most hard-water homes.

    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Tracy

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, and they hold temperature off thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. Our tech cleans the screen, checks sensor values in the service menu and tests thermistor resistance against the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Repair in Tracy, CA

    Whole-house electric tankless units pull a lot of current, often 100 to 150 amps on two or three dedicated breakers. A breaker that trips under load can mean a shorted element or a loose lug that has been arcing, and both get tested with a meter before anything is replaced. We check element resistance and supply voltage. Scorched wire gets cut back.

    Tankless Flush and Scale Removal in Tracy

    Lukewarm water at high flow and a unit that runs loud are two early signs of scale. Most tankless installs include isolation valves with purge ports for exactly this job, and if yours does not, we can add them during the visit so future flushing takes less time. White vinegar will do in a pinch. Commercial descaler works faster.

    Igniter & Flame Rod Repair in Tracy, CA

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. A rod coated in oxide reads low on a microamp meter and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, look for a cracked igniter or a lazy spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Tracy, CA

    Rinnai units are common, and scale causes most of their service calls. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and each part gets tested against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Tracy, CA

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Tracy

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just when it first fires. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, since pressure that sags under load leads to flame loss and slugs of cold water. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Repair in Tracy

    Minor fixes like a flame rod or a flow sensor usually run $150 to $350. A heat exchanger or control board out of warranty can push a repair past $800, so we quote a new unit next to it for comparison. You see the number first.

    Most take one to three hours on site. Descaling alone is closer to an hour and a half, while a part that has to be ordered, like a control board, usually means a second visit within a few business days. You will know before the tech leaves.

    Hard water drops scale inside the heat exchanger every time it heats, and the passages are narrow. Skip the yearly flush and the unit runs hotter, loses output at high flow and eventually locks out on an overheat or scale code, which turns a routine visit into a heat exchanger replacement. The flush takes about an hour.

    Descale yearly on hard water and clean the inlet screen every few months. A softener or scale-reduction cartridge ahead of the heater slows scale a lot, and keeping the intake clear of leaves and lint heads off combustion faults. Check the vent cap after storms.

    Scale is first by a wide margin. After that come dirty flame rods, failed flow sensors and blocked air intakes, with control boards and fan motors showing up less often, usually on units past ten years old. Most of that list can be prevented.

    Temperature swings, a unit that shuts off mid-shower and any code on the display are the usual ones. A rotten egg smell near a gas unit means leave the house and call the gas company.

    Low gas pressure under full load is the most common reason, followed by a dirty flame rod or a blocked intake. Scale that makes the exchanger overheat can do it too. Each gets tested in order.

    We service Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Bosch and AO Smith gas units, plus Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart electrics. Odd or discontinued brands can be harder to source parts for.
