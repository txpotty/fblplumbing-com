    Hot Water Slab Leak Repair in San Francisco, CA

    Hot lines leak under slabs more than cold lines. Heat is the reason. The pipe swells and shrinks against rock every time hot water flows, and a recirculation pump keeps that going constantly, thinning the wall. We repair or reroute the line and can set the recirculation pump on a timer.

    Spot Repair Through the Slab in San Francisco, CA

    Spot repairs fit leaks in otherwise good pipe. One cut, one fix. The floor gets opened, the pipe replaced and tested, and the trench compacted and patched, ready for your flooring contractor.

    Slab Leak Detection in San Francisco

    The first test is the water meter. We shut off everything first. If the meter still turns, we close the water heater inlet and pressure test hot and cold separately, then walk the losing line with a ground mic until the hiss is loudest. Thermal imaging shows hot water leaks as warm areas on the floor.

    Slab Leak Pipe Rerouting in San Francisco

    A reroute abandons the leaking pipe under the slab. The new pipe runs through the attic. For older San Francisco homes, this works well with aging copper or galvanized pipe, since it bypasses every foot of buried line on that run. We keep drywall cuts small at fixture walls.

    Direct Slab Leak Repair in San Francisco, CA

    Direct repair is often the most practical fix for a single leak. A small section of slab gets cut. With the pipe exposed, the damaged length is cut out, new pipe is joined in, and the line is pressure tested before the soil goes back in and gets compacted in layers. The concrete is finished level with the floor.

    Electronic Slab Leak Detection in San Francisco

    Locating comes before cutting. We use a ground mic and a thermal camera. With the mic catching the hiss through the concrete and the camera showing warm water spreading, we can usually narrow the leak to about a foot. We test hot lines, cold lines and drains in your San Francisco home.

    Pipe Rerouting and Repiping in San Francisco

    Old galvanized or thin copper is hard to patch reliably. A reroute bypasses it. We abandon the leaking run in place and install new pipe through walls or the attic, so the problem section is gone from service and the water travels through fresh line that you can reach later. San Francisco homes with original plumbing are good candidates.

    Ground Radar and Acoustic Locating in San Francisco

    Ground-penetrating radar shows what is inside the slab before we cut. It finds rebar and cables. Combined with a listening device, radar finds leaks in any pipe type, even with traffic noise, since radar maps the route and the mic confirms the spot. We use both in San Francisco.
