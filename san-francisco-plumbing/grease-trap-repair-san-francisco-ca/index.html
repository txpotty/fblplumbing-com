    Why Choose FBL Plumbing for Grease Trap Repair in San Francisco, CA?

    Missing outlet tees are the single most common repair we make. Pumpers sometimes knock them off. Without the tee, the outlet draws from the top of the tank, right where the grease floats, and the trap sends grease to the sewer while looking perfectly fine from above. A new tee takes about thirty minutes.

    Preventative Grease Trap Maintenance & Repair in San Francisco, CA

    Most trap repairs we do trace back to pumping intervals that stretched too long. The common rule is to pump when grease and solids fill a quarter of the liquid depth. After a repair, we measure the grease cap and solids with a sludge judge and suggest a pumping schedule based on how fast your trap actually fills. A kitchen running two fryers can hit that mark in a couple of weeks.

    Damaged Baffle Repair in San Francisco

    Baffles are the walls inside the trap that slow the water down long enough for grease to float and solids to sink. Once one cracks or falls loose, the trap is just a box that grease passes through. We pump it out and pull the old baffle. On plastic units we order the factory part for that model number. Steel ones often need a fabricated baffle or a new trap.

    Inlet and Outlet Tee Repair in San Francisco, CA

    Tees are cheap parts with an outsized job. The inlet tee sends flow down below the grease layer. The outlet tee draws water from low in the tank, well under the grease cap, so if it breaks off or someone removes it during pumping, floating grease walks right out to the sewer. New tees go in as Schedule 40 PVC, cut to the drop length the manufacturer or local code specifies.

    Lid, Gasket and Riser Repair in San Francisco, CA

    Outdoor risers take a beating from garbage trucks and the occasional forklift. A cracked riser lets surface water and grit into the interceptor. We replace damaged risers and set traffic-rated covers, H-20 rated where vehicles cross, and seal the riser joints with butyl rope so the tank stays tight. Pedestrian lids in a drive lane are a liability.

    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Trap Repair in San Francisco

    A cable machine punches a hole through grease. A jetter scrubs the whole pipe wall instead. We run a commercial jetter at 3,000 to 4,000 PSI through the line feeding the trap and the line leaving it, working from the cleanout toward the main. On old cast iron we lower the pressure and watch the camera so we do not open up a thin spot.

    Commercial Grease Interceptor Repair in San Francisco, CA

    In-ground gravity interceptors run from about 500 gallons to several thousand, usually precast concrete with two compartments. Over the years, hydrogen sulfide gas softens the concrete above the water line. We pump the tank, inspect the walls and the crossover, and repair spalled concrete with a cementitious patch or an epoxy liner rated for that service. Entry follows confined space rules.

    Grease Trap Leak Repair in San Francisco

    Plastic and fiberglass trap bodies do not rust, but they can crack from a dropped lid or a dishwasher dumping water hotter than the trap is rated for. A hairline crack can sometimes be welded. Anything through the floor of the tank or along a molded seam we treat as a replacement, because patches there fail under the weight of a full trap. You will see the crack yourself before we suggest a fix.

    Baffle Repair and Replacement in San Francisco

    Inside a concrete gravity interceptor the baffle is usually a poured or precast wall with a crossover opening. When that wall spalls, grease slips through to the second compartment. We can rebuild the wall with form work and a sulfate-resistant mix or set a polyethylene baffle panel, depending on the tank. Either way, it needs a full pump-out first.
