    Hard Water and Disposals in San Francisco

    Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running hard. Scale builds up on grind rings and drain tailpieces over the years. Scale makes a disposal noisy. Drainage slows too, since crust narrows the tailpiece and trap, and we check those parts before tying a new unit into them. Heavily scaled parts get replaced the same visit.

    Septic-Friendly Disposal Installs in San Francisco, CA

    Homes on septic can run a disposal, but food solids add load to the tank. Septic systems and private wells are common in rural and mountain parts of Northern California, especially on larger parcels outside city service areas. The Evolution Septic Assist injects an enzyme solution into the grind chamber each time it runs, which helps break down food waste before it reaches the tank. The enzyme cartridge gets mounted, and we show you the refill. Scrape plates first.

    First-Time Disposal Installation in San Francisco

    Kitchens that never had a disposal need some rework. First, the sink gets a disposal flange. The tailpiece and trap have to be reworked, and the cabinet needs a switched outlet, which means an electrician if there is no power under the sink already. Drain slope to the stack gets checked as well.

    Badger Disposal Install in San Francisco

    Most replacement disposals in area kitchens are a Badger. A 1/3 to 3/4 HP Badger handles normal scraps and fits almost any sink base cabinet. The unit hangs on its quick-lock mount, the discharge tube goes on, a cord kit gets added if needed and we finish with a cold-water test grind. About an hour, usually.

    Commercial Disposer Installation in San Francisco, CA

    Restaurant disposals are a different class of machine. Commercial units run 1 to 5 HP, usually on a dedicated circuit, and many need a water solenoid and a control panel mounted on the wall. We mount the unit and plumb the water feed, and your electrician handles the circuit. Health inspectors look at these. Local code may also limit where a disposer can discharge relative to the grease interceptor, so we confirm that before the job starts.

    Batch Feed vs Continuous Feed in San Francisco

    Continuous feed is the type most kitchens have. Flip the switch and it runs until you flip it off. Batch feed only runs with its stopper turned in place, so nobody can reach into a running unit, which some families with small kids prefer and which needs no wall switch at all. We wire each one to match.

    Upfront Disposal Installation Pricing in San Francisco, CA

    Price depends mostly on two things: the unit you pick and how much needs to change under the sink. A like-for-like swap is the low end. A first-time install that needs a new flange and a new outlet costs more, because that is real labor and sometimes an electrician's visit too. We quote the job before any tools come out.

    Standard InSinkErator Installation in San Francisco

    A standard install replaces the unit under an existing sink. We take the old disposal down, scrape the old putty off the flange and set a new flange with fresh putty. Then the mount ring goes on. The unit hangs on the ring, locks with a quarter turn, and the discharge tube meets the trap on a new slip-joint washer. Power comes from a cord kit or the existing hardwire, checked for ground before the breaker goes back on.
