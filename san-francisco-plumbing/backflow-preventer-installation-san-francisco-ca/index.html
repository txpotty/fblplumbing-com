    Residential PVBs usually cost $250 to $700 installed. For bigger homes and businesses, double checks and RPZs run $800 to $3,500 and up, depending on size and whether you need an enclosure.

    Water running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. Other signs include leaks at the body and low pressure past the device, and discolored or foul-smelling water means you should call for an inspection in San Francisco the same day. Do not wait on bad water.

    Annual testing by a certified tester is the norm in San Francisco. It is a separate service from the install, but it keeps the device certified and keeps your account compliant, so we offer reminders and can schedule the test on the same visit as any repair. One call handles both.

    A permit is often required for new assemblies, especially on commercial properties. Local rules get checked, and we pull the permit when required.

    An RPZ protects high-hazard connections where the contaminant could make someone sick. A DCVA covers low-hazard connections with non-toxic contaminants, and the RPZ adds a relief valve that dumps water between its checks, which the double check does not have. Hazard level makes the call.

    In most cases, a plumbing permit is required in San Francisco for a new assembly. We handle the permit on your behalf, and the inspection and test results go to the right offices so the job is fully closed out. No trips to the permit office.
