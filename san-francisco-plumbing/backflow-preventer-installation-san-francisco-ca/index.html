    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in San Francisco

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules permit. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in San Francisco

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in San Francisco, CA usually ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is generally on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    Most residential backflow preventer installations can be completed within 2 to 4 hours. More complex commercial systems or installations requiring significant pipe changes might take longer. We always aim to complete the work efficiently while keeping our quality standards high.

    Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply. Common locations include irrigation system connections, fire sprinkler lines, boiler systems and connections to auxiliary water supplies, usually just after the main water meter or at the point of hazard.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and other equipment.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, particularly if piping must be reconfigured.

    You probably need a backflow preventer if you have an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, a swimming pool or any auxiliary water source that connects to the main water supply. Local regulations in San Francisco often mandate these devices for certain applications to protect public health.

    We install all common types of backflow preventers, including reduced pressure zone assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and double check valve assemblies. Our team will recommend the right device based on your property's hazard level and local plumbing codes in San Francisco.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This verifies the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also help with scheduling these annual tests.
