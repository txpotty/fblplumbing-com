    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Repair in Folsom

    Most simple toilet repairs, a new flapper or fill valve, cost about $125 to $275 installed. Resetting a toilet on a new wax ring usually runs $175 to $350, and a flange repair can bring the total to $300 to $500. The price is set before we start.

    Repeat clogs usually come from wipes, toys, too much paper per flush, or an early low-flow toilet that never moved waste well. Other times the trouble is further down the line, like a sagging drain, a crushed pipe, roots in the sewer or a blocked vent that keeps the bowl from draining with full force. Cameras settle the question.

    Flush only paper, and keep wipes, floss, paper towels and cotton swabs in the trash where they belong. Deal with a running toilet as soon as you notice it, because the leak wears the flush valve seat rougher over time and turns a cheap flapper job into a full flush valve replacement. Check the supply line once a year.

    Flapper and fill valve jobs take twenty to forty-five minutes. Pulling the toilet and resetting it on a new wax ring takes about an hour, and a flange repair adds another thirty to sixty minutes depending on what is under the floor. A clog past the trap can take longer.

    A failed wax ring is the usual cause, often helped along by a toilet that rocks or a flange that has cracked or rusted through. Condensation can fool you, though. On a humid day, the tank sweats and drips down the outside, which looks like a base leak but is not one.

    Low water in the tank is the first thing we check, followed by a partly closed angle stop, scale in the rim jets, a clogged siphon jet and a partial clog in the trapway. Any one of those can do it. We find which one before replacing parts, since a new flapper will not help a scaled jet.

    Swapping tank parts or resetting a toilet on a new wax seal generally does not need a permit. Moving the drain or adding a new toilet usually does, and the rules come from local code, so they vary from one town to the next even within Northern California. We look it up before work begins.

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber, Mansfield and Delta cover most of what we see, and we stock their common flappers and fill valves. Older brands like Eljer and Briggs are fine too. Parts for some discontinued models take a day or two to arrive.
