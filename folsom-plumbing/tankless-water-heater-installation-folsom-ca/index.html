    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Folsom, CA

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. The vent changes as well. We plan both on the quote.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Folsom, CA

    Replacing an old tankless unit tends to go faster than a conversion. Since the gas line and vent are already there, most swaps mean matching connections, inspecting the old vent and mounting the new heater. Different brands vent differently. We check before ordering.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Folsom

    Propane homes in Folsom can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. A small tank can run short.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Folsom

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. Less water goes down the drain.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Folsom

    Upgrading from a tank in Folsom begins with your current setup: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits.     Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Folsom, CA

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Folsom, CA homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Folsom

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and many models include a built-in recirculation pump. That pump can push hot water toward a far bathroom on a schedule, which shortens the wait at the tap without adding a separate pump under the sink. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC.
