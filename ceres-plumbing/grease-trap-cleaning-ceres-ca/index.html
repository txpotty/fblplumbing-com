    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Ceres

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. A large interceptor with a hardened grease cap can run past $1,000. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    It depends on trap size and menu. Commercial kitchens usually land between every two weeks and every three months. Homes with a small unit may go a year, but a fryer-heavy kitchen on a 50-gallon trap might need pumping every other week.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Leave it long enough and the grease cap sets up like a brick. The hydrogen sulfide from rotting grease eats concrete too, and a neglected interceptor can need new baffles and risers years early.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    There is no parts warranty on a pump-out, since nothing gets installed. We do guarantee the work itself. Should it drain poorly after our visit, or a backup trace back to something we missed, the crew returns and makes it right.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Most people use trap for the small indoor unit, usually under 100 gallons. Interceptor usually means the large tank in the ground outside, often 500 gallons and up, and codes size the two in different ways.

    It is the most common pumping standard. Pump before grease and solids fill a quarter of the depth. Past that point the water moves through too fast for grease to separate, so grease rides out the outlet even though the trap looks like it is working.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. The disposal is the part that gets people fined. Many programs want a licensed hauler's record for every cleaning, and a kitchen doing its own has nothing to show the inspector.
