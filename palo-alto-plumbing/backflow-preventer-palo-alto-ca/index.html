    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Palo Alto

    Annual residential testing runs $75 to $150 per assembly. Repairs with a rebuild kit typically run $150 to $450. Residential installs run about $300 to $1,200, while a commercial RPZ is often $1,500 to $5,000 installed with its drain and enclosure.

    Water providers may send follow-up notices, charge fees or eventually suspend service. Booking early heads all of that off. We can often fit urgent tests in on short notice, even when the deadline is only a few days away.

    A standard test usually takes 20 to 45 minutes per assembly. Water is briefly shut off during the test, so we let you know before we close the valve on the supply side. Repairs may add an hour or more.

    A brass assembly often lasts 10 to 20 years, sometimes longer. The rubber parts inside wear out much sooner, which is why rebuild kits exist and why most failed tests are fixed without replacing the body. Freezes shorten that life.

    Yearly testing is the norm for water utilities. That schedule exists because rubber parts wear and debris settles on the seats, and neither one is visible from outside the valve. We send reminders.

    Constant dripping from the relief valve, low water pressure and visible cracks are the usual signs. Odd-colored water is another sign. Prompt repair keeps you in compliance and keeps the water provider from sending a second notice with a new deadline attached.

    Most water providers, including those around Palo Alto, require a test once a year. Many also want a test after installation, after any repair and whenever the assembly gets relocated. Check your notice for the deadline.

    Look for a steady leak from the device, a sudden drop in water pressure or discolored water at the tap. On an RPZ, water spitting from the relief port every time pressure changes is a sign a check is leaking back. Have it looked at.
