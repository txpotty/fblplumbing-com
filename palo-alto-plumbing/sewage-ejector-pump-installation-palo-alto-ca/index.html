    Why Choose FBL Plumbing for Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Palo Alto, CA?

    Installing pumps in Northern California homes means dealing with whatever is under the slab, from rock to wet clay. Expansive clay soils are common across Northern California, and seasonal swelling and shrinking can shift slabs and buried sewer laterals. We plan the dig for that. We bed and backfill each basin so it will not float or move, then patch the slab with concrete over a vapor barrier instead of leaving a loose lid.

    • Alarm Protection, A high water alarm sounds before waste reaches the floor.

    Citywide Coverage

    From older homes with toilets below grade to new custom builds with lower-level suites, we install ejectors all over Palo Alto. Many older pits are open or poorly sealed and leak sewer gas, so a gasketed, vented lid is often the first fix we suggest. Odor is a warning.

    Experienced Team

    Our team reads the pump curve, not a rule of thumb. Lift, run length, fittings and discharge size all change a pump's output, and a pump chosen without that math tends to short-cycle or strain. Math beats guessing.

    Safety-First Approach

    Ejector pumps sit in water below grade, so electrical safety matters. We make sure the pump is on its own properly grounded circuit with the protection code requires, and that the alarm has a separate power source so a tripped pump breaker does not also silence the alarm. Separate power is safer.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Palo Alto

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so every fixture drains to a sealed pit instead of flowing by gravity. We cut the slab, trench the drain lines to the basin, set it, run a two-inch vent up through the house to the roof and pipe the discharge to the building drain with a check valve and shutoff.     Ejector Pump Replacement in Palo Alto

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Palo Alto, CA

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes may need two-pump setups with an alternating control panel. Alternating the lead pump spreads wear, and when one fails or flow spikes, the second starts on its own and the panel sounds an alarm. Redundancy matters here.
