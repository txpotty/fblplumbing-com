    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in San Rafael

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Pilot Light Repair in San Rafael

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Figure on roughly an hour for most pilot repairs.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in San Rafael

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your San Rafael home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in San Rafael

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Every suspect part then gets tested with a meter.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in San Rafael, CA

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. Plenty of leaks come from the drain valve, the relief valve or a sweating supply nipple. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a San Rafael, CA home. If the bottom seam is wet, the tank has failed. Nothing patches that.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in San Rafael

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. That usually means a relief valve or a partly closed supply valve.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in San Rafael

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.
