    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. It takes a minute.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the check valve, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. It takes under an hour.
