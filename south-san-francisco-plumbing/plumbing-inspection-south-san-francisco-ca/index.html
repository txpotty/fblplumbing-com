    Whole-Home Plumbing Inspections in South San Francisco

    Our whole-home inspections focus on the problems we see most in Northern California houses. Common finds include old pipe, stiff shutoffs, tired heaters, slow drains and rooty or sagging sewers. We test each one directly. Catching a corroded fitting or a partial sewer blockage now keeps it from becoming a ceiling stain or a backup on a holiday weekend.

    Water Heater Safety Inspection in South San Francisco

    A water heater stores a lot of hot water under pressure, so safety items matter. Our checks include the relief valve and its discharge line, the expansion tank and the drain pan. We check gas units for draft. A heater that backdrafts can spill combustion gases into the house, so we test the draft at the vent hood and note any problems right away.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in South San Francisco, CA

    Many older homes have clay, cast iron or Orangeburg sewer lines that can crack, sag or fill with roots. We run a high-resolution camera from a cleanout to the city connection or septic tank. The video shows offsets, bellies, roots and breaks along with their depth and location, and you keep a copy of the recording for your records or for a contractor.

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in South San Francisco

    Water hardness in Northern California varies a lot by source, with snowmelt-fed systems running soft and many inland groundwater systems running mineral-heavy. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. Signs include crusted aerators, clogged showerheads, slow toilet fill and heater sediment, and we can test hardness during the visit. You get options, not a pitch. A softener, a filter or a scale-reducing system can extend heater and fixture life, and we tell you which one fits the hardness we measured.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in South San Francisco

    A standard plumbing inspection in South San Francisco, CA usually costs $150 to $350. Size changes the price. That fee covers the supply pipe, fixtures, water heater, drains and pressure check, while a sewer camera run is usually priced as an add-on because it takes extra time and equipment. You get the price when you book.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It just goes only so far. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    Plan on one to two hours for most residential inspections. Larger homes or long sewer lines can take a bit longer.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Noisy drains are a clue. A damp wall, a musty cabinet or a warm spot on the floor can mean a hidden leak, and an inspection finds it before it spreads.

    For an older home, it usually pays for itself. Age brings hidden issues. Homes with galvanized supply pipe, clay or cast iron sewers or original water heaters often have problems that are invisible from the outside, and an inspection lets you plan repairs instead of reacting to a failure.

    We inspect all the common brands and materials. That includes Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White heaters. We work with PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC, cast iron, galvanized and poly, and we know the usual weak points for each and for common fixture brands.

    Rising water bills, low pressure, discolored water, slow drains, sewer odors and warm spots on floors are all good reasons. Homes with original plumbing also benefit from one.

    A plumbing inspection is a deeper look at one system. General inspectors check basic function. A general home inspector runs faucets and flushes toilets, while our plumbers run sewer cameras, test pressure, identify pipe types and flag code issues that fall outside a general inspection.
