    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Livermore

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Livermore, mostly in basements and utility closets. Their parts are simple, and a universal thermocouple fits nearly all of them. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. One goes in while we are there.

    Bradford White and Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Livermore, CA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Livermore, CA. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. We keep both on the truck. Using factory parts keeps your tank warranty valid.

    Repair or Replace: Straight Answers in Livermore, CA

    Before any wrench turns, you get a written price for the repair and an honest read on the tank in your Livermore, CA home. A bad thermocouple on a six-year-old tank is an easy repair to justify. A fourteen-year-old tank with a rusty flue, a leaking bottom and a failed gas valve is a different story, and putting a $500 control on it rarely makes sense. You will hear plainly which side of that line your heater falls on. The decision is yours.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Livermore, CA

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. We check the thermostat setting, the immersion sensor and the valve's outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.

    Pilot Light Repair in Livermore

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Gas Water Heater Repair Cost in Livermore, CA

    Thermocouple and pilot repairs usually run $150 to $350. Gas control valves often come in at $350 to $700 installed. Power vent blower work runs $400 to $800, and a full replacement with a new gas tank generally costs $1,600 to $3,000 depending on size, venting and whether the install needs code updates. Tank age drives the advice. On a tank older than twelve years, you get both prices side by side.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Livermore

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. The usual failures are the blower motor, the pressure switch and the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. The control's error code is our starting point. Every suspect part then gets tested with a meter.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Livermore, CA

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.
