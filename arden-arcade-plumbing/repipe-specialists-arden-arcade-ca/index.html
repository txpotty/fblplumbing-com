    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Arden-Arcade

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. Clogged aerators are a clue. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest.     PEX Pipe Replacement in Arden-Arcade, CA

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Because PEX flexes, it absorbs some of the shock that causes water hammer, and long runs with few fittings mean fewer places for a leak to start inside a wall. We keep PEX out of sunlight and away from water heater flues.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Arden-Arcade

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. We use Type L, not the thinner Type M, and we support it with the right hangers so it does not rub or rattle in the walls. Lead-free solder only. Because some water chemistries eat copper, we look at your leak history before recommending it over PEX.

    PEX Repiping in Arden-Arcade, CA

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. PEX shrugs off scale and chlorine, survives freezing better than copper and installs faster, so it is our most common repipe material. We use PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter of the pipe open. That keeps pressure up at the far fixtures.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Arden-Arcade

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Expansive clay soils are common across Northern California, and seasonal swelling and shrinking can crack slabs and buried sewer laterals. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.
