    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Richmond

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Whole-home repiping usually requires a permit and inspection. Our office files the permit and books the inspection.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty on the repipe, and pipe makers offer their own long-term warranties. We explain coverage before work begins.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. Homes built from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s are most likely to have it.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. Uponor is our usual pick. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. Licensed crews work faster and leave you warranties and paperwork.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. In water that does not attack it, Type L copper can last 50 years or more, and both are major upgrades over poly.
