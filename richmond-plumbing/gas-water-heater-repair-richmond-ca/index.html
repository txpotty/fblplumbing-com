    Propane Water Heater Repair in Richmond

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Richmond, CA

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. The burner comes out, the ports get brushed and the orifice gets cleared. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner send us to check the flue baffle next.
