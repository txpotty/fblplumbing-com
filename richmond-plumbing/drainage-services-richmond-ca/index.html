    We do not recommend using chemical drain cleaners. They can be corrosive, potentially damaging your pipes, especially older ones, and are often ineffective against tough clogs.     We recommend skipping them. Harsh chemicals can damage older pipes and rarely remove scale or roots. A cable or jetter clears the line safely and completely.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Larger projects with dry wells or extensive trenching can take longer. We give you a timeline along with your estimate.

    A French drain collects water that soaks into soil and is best along foundations and low areas. A channel drain catches water flowing across hard surfaces like driveways and patios. Plenty of properties benefit from both.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains and dry wells require correct slope, fabric and outlet placement, and mistakes can push water toward the house. Professional installation also handles utility locating and any permits.

    Tree roots are instinctively attracted to the moisture and nutrients found inside sewer lines.
