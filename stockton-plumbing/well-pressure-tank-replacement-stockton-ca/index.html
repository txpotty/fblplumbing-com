    Pressure Switch Replacement in Stockton, CA

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. Worn contacts can fail to open or close. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, since a new tank paired with a tired switch is asking for trouble. Cheap part.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Stockton, CA

    Diaphragm tanks use a flexible sheet clamped inside the shell. The diaphragm splits air from water. When it fails, symptoms match a bladder tank, with short cycling, pressure swings and water spitting from the air valve. Replacement is the usual answer.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Stockton

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. First step, kill the breaker to the pump. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and connect it with new fittings and fresh sealant. Usually a morning.

    Galvanized Steel Pressure Tank Upgrades in Stockton

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Air and water share the same space. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, which means draining it to restore the air. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Stockton

    Listen for the switch clicking every few seconds. That fast on-off pattern means the tank has no usable air, and the pump is now taking every hit of demand directly, which wears the pump and the switch contacts. We swap the tank and the cycling stops.

    Preventative Well Pressure Tank Maintenance in Stockton, CA

    Once a year, test the pre-charge. Shut off the pump, drain the system to zero, and put a tire gauge on the valve at the top of the tank, topping it up to two psi below the cut-in if it reads low. Water out of the valve means a torn bladder.

    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in Stockton, CA

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Switch and tank are a team. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, and a cheap new gauge beats guessing. Small parts, big role.
