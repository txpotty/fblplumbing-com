    Leaking Drain Pipe Repair in Stockton, CA

    Drain leaks are low pressure, so they drip instead of spray. Most go unnoticed for months. Corrosion, shifting framing and cracked fittings are the usual causes, and we trace the leak by running each fixture and watching the joints, then replace the bad length with pipe that matches what is already there. Every repair is done to code.

    Storm Drain Repair in Stockton, CA

    Yard and storm drains carry rain away from the foundation. Strong winter storms can bring days of heavy rain to Northern California, saturating ground and pushing water into basements and sewer lines. Debris and silt fill them up. We clear the line with a jetter, camera it to look for crushed or separated sections, and repair broken pipe so the runoff reaches daylight instead of soaking the soil next to your foundation. A clean outlet matters too.

    Vent Pipe Repair in Stockton

    Sewer gas leaking into a room can point to a missing vent. Old renovations often skipped it. Bird nests, debris and roofing work can block existing vents, while fixtures that never had a vent can sometimes get an air admittance valve if code allows. Proper venting protects every trap in the house.

    Exterior Drain Tile Repair in Stockton, CA

    Drain tile, sometimes called a French drain, carries groundwater away from the footing. Fine soil plugs it over time. Expansive clay soils are common across Northern California, and seasonal swelling and shrinking can crack slabs and buried sewer laterals. When a tile line is crushed, clogged or was laid without fabric, water builds up against the foundation, so we camera the tile, find the failed section and replace it with perforated pipe wrapped in filter fabric and clean gravel. We confirm it drains to daylight or a sump.

    Tub and Shower Drain Repair in Stockton, CA

    Hair, soap scum and minerals narrow bath drains over time. Drain seals wear out. We replace worn pop-up assemblies, broken tub shoes and leaking shower drain bodies, and failed drain gaskets can usually be replaced from above without cutting into the ceiling below. Any access hole is kept small and neat.
