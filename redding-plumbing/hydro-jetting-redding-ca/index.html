    Hydro Jetting Before Pipe Lining in Redding, CA

    Liners need clean pipe. Buildup keeps them from sealing. We jet the host pipe to remove roots, scale and debris, then camera it to confirm it is ready, cutting out any heavy obstructions before the liner goes in, since skipping prep can lead to wrinkles or poor bonding.

    High-Pressure Water Jetting for Drain Cleaning in Redding

    Our jetters run up to 4,000 PSI. That breaks apart hard buildup. The same jet that clears grease from PVC can descale cast iron, as long as the pressure matches the pipe.

    Kitchen Line Grease Jetting in Redding, CA

    Grease clings and hardens. Flat runs are the worst. We use smaller hose and nozzles for kitchen branches, which scrub the grease off the walls rather than tunneling through it, so your sink and dishwasher drain properly for much longer.

    Root Cutting With Hydro Jetting in Redding

    Specialty nozzles slice fine roots. Cables miss those. Jetting removes root hairs left by a cable and exposes each joint where roots get in, which shows up clearly on camera, which helps you weigh lining against a spot repair.

    Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking Cost Comparison in Redding, CA

    Snaking costs less up front. Jetting usually holds up better. A cable punches through the blockage and leaves residue on the walls, which brings the clog back, while jetting cleans the whole pipe and cuts down the number of service calls over time.

    Preventive Maintenance Jetting in Redding

    Repeat clogs suggest a maintenance plan. It keeps the line clear. Homes usually need jetting every year or two, while busy kitchens may need it quarterly to stay ahead of grease, and each visit includes a camera check to catch new problems early.

    Camera Inspection and Hydro Jetting in Redding, CA

    The camera goes in before the jetter. We never skip it. Seeing the line first lets us pick settings that clean well and protect the pipe, and it confirms jetting is the right call.

    Scale Removal in Cast Iron Pipes in Redding

    Cast iron grows rust tubercles. Minerals make it worse. Jetting with the right nozzle can strip much of this buildup and restore flow, while we keep the pressure in a safe range for aged iron and watch the camera for thin spots, which is also the standard prep before pipe lining.
